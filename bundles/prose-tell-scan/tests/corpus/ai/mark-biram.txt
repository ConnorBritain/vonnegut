Mark Biram

Mark Biram is a British academic and writer whose work focuses on the cultural, political, and gendered dimensions of football in Latin America, with a particular emphasis on women’s football in Brazil and Colombia. He is known for his academic publications, public scholarship, and collaborations with football journalists such as Tim Vickery.

Early Life and Education

Biram earned his undergraduate and postgraduate degrees in the United Kingdom at Manchester, Newcastle and Bristol respectively, specializing in Latin American studies, gender studies, and sport. He most recently completed a PhD at the University of Bristol focusing on women's football in Brazil and Colombia, incorporating ethnographic fieldwork and feminist theoretical frameworks.

Career

Biram is the author of Women’s Football in Brazil and Colombia: South American Feminisms, Resistance and the Beautiful Game (Routledge, 2024), which has been widely cited in sports studies and praised for its contribution to the emerging literature on gender and football in Latin America.

He has contributed chapters to several edited volumes, including Women’s Football in Brazil: Social Challenges and Historical Perspectives, Fútbol: Why Soccer Matters in Latin America, and the Routledge Handbook of Sport in Latin America.

Biram is also the Principal Investigator on Girls on the Pitch, a project funded by the UK Arts and Humanities Research Council (AHRC), which explores the development of women’s football cultures and leadership across Latin America. The project received both seed and full-scale funding across two rounds.

Writing and Public Engagement

Beyond academia, Biram is known for his accessible writing style and public-facing work. He has co-authored Mundiales: A South American History of the World Cup (1924–2022) with Tim Vickery, forthcoming from Pitch Publishing. The book explores each men’s FIFA World Cup from the perspective of South American nations, blending historical narrative with cultural and political analysis.

He has appeared in interviews, podcasts, and public talks on topics such as football and feminism, the legacy of Pelé, and the struggles for professionalisation in South American women’s leagues.
