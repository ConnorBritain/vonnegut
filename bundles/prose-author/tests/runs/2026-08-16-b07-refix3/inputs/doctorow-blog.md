# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

Ten usable samples, all with `source`, `date`, `human_authored: true`; none excluded; `voice.md` is empty, so everything below is derived from the corpus. Scope for every count: the essay body of all ten samples — the text after the first `(permalink)` line and before the next furniture `(permalink)` line — with bare-URL lines and the one `(Image: …)` credit removed as publication furniture. That is **17,418 words**.

## 1. Cadence

Long sentences that stack clauses, punctuated by very short ones. There are 732 sentences, mean 23.8 words; 88 of them run to eight words or fewer and 90 run to forty or more — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (88 short sentences, 5.05 per 1,000 words). Both extremes appear in every sample. The short one is where the judgement lands: after two paragraphs building the "consumer welfare" argument, `2026-07-16-lucky-orifices` gives the whole thing *"QED."* **If a sentence has run past forty words of accumulating clauses, make the next one under eight and flat.**

The paragraph turns on a sentence-initial *But*. Counting only *But* at the start of a sentence: 36 instances — 9/10 samples, several times per piece, 2.07 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"But workers can't simply refuse offers and wait for the wage on offer to increase."*).

Consecutive sentences reuse an opening frame. Counting runs of two or more consecutive sentences inside one paragraph that open on the same two words: 16 runs — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece, 0.92 per 1,000 words. The rule is strict and misses parallel sets that swap the verb.

## 2. How a piece opens

I checked all ten; they fall into three kinds with nothing left over.

Four open on a flat declarative that states the verdict or defines the subject before any evidence — 4/10 (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*).

Four open on a deictic pointer that ends on a colon and hands off to a quotation or a link — 4/10 (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Here's an irony: the 'gig economy' is a statistical black hole."*).

Two open in someone else's words, an epigraph or a borrowed coinage — 2/10 (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"I often wonder what the Vintners buy/One half so precious as the Goods they sell"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

Seven end on a flat declarative judgement — no summary, no exhortation, no widening out — 7/10 (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"The fact that our political classes seem to think that they can choose the time and manner of their passing is baffling."*).

The other three end by handing off rather than concluding: one on a rhetorical question, two on a colon into a link — 3/10.

The close returns to the word the piece has been contesting and redefines it against its owner. Counted strictly — a double-quoted span in the final paragraph that also occurs earlier in the body — 3/10 samples (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires"*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

**Second person, constantly.** Counting *you, your, yours, yourself, yourselves*: 390 instances — 10/10 samples, throughout, 22.39 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"how can you disenshittify your internet experience?"*). The reader is addressed from outside, as the person things are being done to.

**And a *we* that includes the reader.** Counting *we, us, our, ours, ourselves*: 163 instances — 10/10 samples, throughout, 9.36 per 1,000 words. The corpus runs both stances at once: a *you* who must act and an *us* the opposition is doing it to.

**Contraction is near-total.** Counting `n't`, `'re`, `'ve`, `'ll`, `'m`, `'d`, and `'s` only in the elisions *it's, that's, there's, here's, what's, who's, he's, she's, let's* — not possessives: 301 instances — 10/10 samples, throughout, 17.28 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"It's not exceptionally wicked. It's not exceptionally good."*). Against them, **only 10 uncontracted negations in all 17,418 words** (*does not* ×2, *do not* ×2, *should not* ×2, *will not*, *need not*, *is not*, *would not*), and 4 of those 10 sit inside block quotes from other writers — **6 in his own prose**. **Do not write *is not*, *cannot*, *does not* or *it is*; he does not.**

**He appears as a person, not only as an arguer.** Counting *I, me, my, mine, myself*: 109 instances — 10/10 samples, several times per piece, 6.26 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"I'm 55."*). His age, his cancer, his kid, his old bookselling job.

**Claims are unhedged; the concession is a separate sentence.** Counting *of course* anywhere plus sentence-initial *Sure,* / *Admittedly,* / *Granted,* / *To be fair,* / *True,*: 20 instances — 9/10 samples, once or twice per piece, 1.15 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Sure, Canada's imposed some retaliatory tariffs on US products"*). **Grant the point in its own sentence, then reverse it with a sentence-initial *But*; do not soften the claim itself.**

**He tells the reader what to do.** Counting sentence-initial imperatives from a stated list, excluding the subordinator *Just as*: 20 instances — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece, 1.15 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Stop using Chrome, switch to Firefox:"*).

**Profanity is a verdict, not decoration.** Counting the list *shit, shitty, shittiness, bullshit, enshittify, fuck, fucking, fucked, asshole, piss, pissed, screw, damned, horny, balls, crap* — excluding *dickover(s)*, a quoted coinage that is the subject of one post: 25 instances — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece, 1.44 per 1,000 words. It lands on the judgement (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"It's bullshit."*), not on the description.

**The opposition is named, never abstract.** Counting capitalised tokens that are not the first word of a sentence: 682 — 10/10 samples, throughout, 39.15 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Carney dropped his plan to charge US tech companies a 3% tax."*).

**Allies get the floor by name.** Counting a capitalised name of one or two words immediately followed by a reporting verb from a stated list; the rule also catches institutions, and four of the 29 are institutions: 29 instances — 7/10 samples, several times per piece, 1.66 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"As Lina Khan likes to say, companies that are too big to fail become too big to jail"*). The argument is built by extending a named person's formulation, not by asserting alone.

## 5. Figures

**Images are drawn from monarchy, aristocracy and religion.** I cannot bound "a figure", so this is not a count of figures: it is a count of the vocabulary they are built from, under the word list *king(s), monarch(s), royal, royalty, crown, throne, prince(s), aristocracy, aristocrat, feudal, lord(s), hereditary, dynasty, oligarchy, admiral(s), pirate(s), god(s), god-botherers, deity, demiurge, divine, providence, puritan(s), elect, eschaton, satanic, handmaiden* — 48 instances, 2.76 per 1,000 words, 7/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"every pirate yearns to be an admiral"*). **Use that vocabulary at about this rate.**

**Figures are borrowed whole from named stories and dropped in unexplained** — Les Misérables, Omelas, King Arthur, Renfield, Mr Creosote, the Rubáiyát, the Hitchhiker's leopard — 8/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"a locked filing cabinet stuck in a disused lavatory with a sign on the door saying 'Beware of the Leopard'"*). No rate: I could not write a rule that decides what counts as an allusion and a stranger would not reproduce my number.

**One analogy is opened and then run for several paragraphs**, with the mechanism worked through before the argument resumes — the tipped-minimum restaurant, the picks-and-shovels wholesaler, the murder offset — 7/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed"*). No rate: I could not bound where an analogy starts and stops.

**The compound is the unit of coinage.** Counting hyphenated compounds under the rule *lowercase run of two or more letters, hyphen, lowercase run of two or more letters*: 173 instances across 146 distinct forms — 10/10 samples, throughout, 9.93 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"the word-guessing program"*). **Coin at about this rate.**

## 6. Register range

**What moves is second-person density.** The two service pieces carry 98 and 77 second-person tokens against 21–32 in the other eight, and 8 of the 20 imperatives — 2/10 samples. When the piece is telling you how to do something, the *you* roughly triples and the imperatives arrive in runs.

**The first person swells when the subject is mortality.** `2026-07-14-designated-survivor` carries 32 *I/me/my* tokens against a corpus mean of 10.9 — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (*"my doctor just informed me that I am cancer free"*).

**What does not move:** contraction, the colon hinge, the en dash and the named-source attribution hold at similar rates in all ten regardless of subject or register — 10/10 samples.

## 7. What the corpus never does

**The mid-sentence swerve is a spaced en dash.** 72 spaced en dashes — 10/10 samples, several times per piece, 4.13 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"The difference between the left and the right isn't the identities of the ruling class – it's whether we have a ruling class at all."*). Against them: **0 em dashes and 0 double hyphens in all 17,418 words.**

**Structure is carried by a colon that hands off to a citation.** 112 of the 298 body paragraphs end on a colon — 10/10 samples, throughout, 6.43 per 1,000 words (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"Austerity is fascism's best recruiting tool:"*). A claim is asserted, the paragraph ends on the colon, the evidence follows. Against that, in all ten bodies: **0 headings, 0 bulleted lists, 0 numbered lists.** New subjects arrive as a short paragraph-opening sentence that names them — *"This is where DROP comes in."*

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is empty, so nothing here is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

All ten are daily posts from one blog inside a 16-day window, addressed to a returning readership that already accepts the premises. Nothing shows how this voice behaves in another medium, or toward a reader who disagrees, or toward a stranger — 10/10 samples.

Body lengths run 893 to 2,538 words. Nothing here shows the voice under about 900 words, which matters because several habits above are per-1,000-word rates measured on pieces roughly twice that length — 10/10 samples.

These are scrapes. I treated bare-URL lines and one image credit as furniture and excluded them from all counts — 10/10 samples.

Five observations were dropped for want of a citation or a reproducible counting rule.
