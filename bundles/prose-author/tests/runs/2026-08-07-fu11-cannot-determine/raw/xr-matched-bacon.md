Men purpose greatly and perform little; for the purpose is made once, in an hour of clearness, but the life is made a hundred times a day, in hours of no clearness at all. A resolution is a single act of the mind, whereas a habit is the mind's standing posture; and the posture will outlast the act. Therefore he who has resolved and not altered anything he does daily has resolved nothing, but only enjoyed the sensation of resolving.

The intention is loud and the habit is quiet, which is why we mistake the one for the other. Intention announces itself, keeps anniversaries, requires witnesses; the small habit asks for none of this and goes about its work unattended. Ask a man what he means to become and he will tell you at length. Ask him what he did before breakfast and he will say it was nothing in particular. That nothing in particular is the greater part of him.

Character is not laid down as a wall is built, in a season, with the sound of hammers; it is laid down as silt is laid down, in quantities too small to be seen on the day they arrive, so that the shape of the ground is settled long before anyone thinks to survey it. Therefore judge a man not by what he has decided but by what he no longer decides — by the actions that have passed out of his deliberation altogether, for those are the ones that will still be there when his deliberation is tired.

There is a common error, that great character is made by great occasions. The occasion does not make the man; it only asks him what he has been practising. A man who has been careful in small monies will not steal a great one, and a man loose in small truths will not hold a great one, though he is sincerely astonished at himself when the moment comes. He had been in training the whole time and did not know for what.

The small habit has this further advantage over the intention, that it costs almost nothing and therefore survives almost anything. Grand programmes of reform are expensive and are given up the first week the man is ill or unhappy; the trivial practice, being trivial, is kept through illness and unhappiness both, and is still standing when the reform has been forgotten. So the way to change a life is not to resolve more strenuously but to resolve more narrowly, and to choose a thing so small that no bad day is an excuse against it.

Yet the same law that builds also erodes, and it works with the same patience and the same discretion. No man is corrupted by a decision. He is corrupted by a small allowance made in a tired moment, which is made again because it was made once, and a third time because twice is a habit and habits are not examined. The first concession is argued for; the second is merely remembered; the third is not noticed at all. Let a man watch the second one, therefore, for the first is honest and by the third it is too late.

Nor is it any use to hate the habit while keeping it, for hatred is another intention, and intentions have already been weighed here and found wanting. The old husbandmen knew that a field is not improved by being resented. It is improved by being worked a little, and then again a little, in weather no one would have chosen.
