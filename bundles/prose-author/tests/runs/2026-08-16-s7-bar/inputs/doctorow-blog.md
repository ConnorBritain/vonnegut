# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

Derived from ten blog posts (2026-07-09 to 2026-07-24), 17,549 body words. Throughout, *body* means the text between the first `(permalink)` line and the boilerplate sections, including the standalone link lines; `per 1,000 words` uses that 17,549. Counts are given only for habits that recur *inside* a piece — a once-per-piece structural move carries its sample count alone.

## 1. Cadence

Sentences are long and the variation is wide: 582 sentences, mean 30.0 words, median 25, and 147 of them run 40 words or longer — 10/10 samples, throughout (`sample 2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"They should understand that profits are only good when you're a scrappy challenger, but once you've conquered the market, every capitalist seeks to become a feudal lord, converting profits to rents and insulating themselves from an exhausting life of constant competition"*). Counting rule: sentences split on `.!?` followed by whitespace and a capital, quote or bracket, with standalone URL lines and the bracketed image credit removed and single-letter abbreviations rejoined.

The long sentence is then stopped dead. Counting a sentence of seven words or fewer whose immediately preceding sentence runs twenty words or more, there are 28 such stops — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: a 62-word sentence about pirates and admirals, then *"They're right, of course."*; `2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: a 47-word sentence, then *"Because we let them."*). **If a sentence has run past forty words, make the next one five words and flat, and let it be the judgement.**

Paragraphs are short: 298 of them across the ten pieces, mean 58 words — about two sentences — 10/10 samples, throughout. The argument advances by paragraph, not by section.

Paragraphs open on a connective: 33 begin with *But, And, So, Sure, Now, Meanwhile, Instead, Nevertheless, Likewise, Then, Yet, Still, Of course* or *Or* — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"But that's not true – there's even more money to be made wholesaling picks and shovels…"*).

## 2. How a piece opens

The ten openings fall into three kinds; I checked all ten and they account for all ten.

A flat declarative verdict on the subject, delivered before any evidence — 4/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*; `2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"As a technology, AI isn't exceptional."*).

A presentational frame or an epigraph that hands the first sentence to somebody else — 4/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Here's a sentence that stopped me in my tracks last week:"*, followed by the quotation and its link; `2026-07-13-go-meta-meta` opens on the Rubáiyát).

The opposing position stated in its own best terms, untouched — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"The theory of markets goes like this: even the best of us can fall prey to selfishness and rationalization…"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The last paragraph picks up a word the piece has been holding in quotation marks and redefines it against whoever supplied it — 5/10 samples (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"But the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires and convinces them that they are above any discipline or rules."*; `2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"they value their flexibility"*). The other five end on a rhetorical question, on a flat judgement, or on a link.

Two of ten end on a colon and a bare link rather than on a sentence — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Trump has made the case for abandoning these deals better than I ever could have:"*).

Three of ten print a bracketed image credit as the final line after the closing sentence — 3/10 samples (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"(Image: Kanerva T, CC BY 4.0, modified)"*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

Second person carries the argument: 390 tokens of *you / your / yours / yourself / yourselves*, case-insensitive, URL lines excluded — 10/10 samples, throughout. The corpus slides between the reader addressed directly and a generic *you* standing for anyone, often inside one paragraph, so both are counted together (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"I can leave an apartment building empty while you freeze to death on its sidewalk"*).

There is also a *we* that includes the reader, and the two stances divide the labour rather than compete: 163 tokens of *we / us / our / ours / ourselves* — 10/10 samples, throughout. *We* is the polity that could decide otherwise; *you* is the person being done to (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"because they can, and we don't stop them"*). **Use *we* for what could be legislated and *you* for what is being done to a body.**

The writer appears as a person, not only as an arguer: 109 tokens of *I / me / my / mine / myself* (capitalised *I* only) — 10/10 samples, several times per piece — including age, illness and a dead friend (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"I'm 55."*).

Contracted throughout: 193 contractions, counting `n't`, `'re`, `'ve`, `'ll`, `'m` and `'d` with the ASCII apostrophe and excluding `'s` entirely, since it cannot be told from the possessive mechanically — 10/10 samples, throughout. Against those 193 there are 9 uncontracted negations (*do not*, *is not*, *cannot* and the like) in the whole corpus.

The opponent's vocabulary is held at arm's length in quotation marks: 163 double-quoted spans of five words or fewer — 10/10 samples, throughout (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"what Uber calls 'flexibility' is really a way for the company to offload its operational risks onto its drivers"*). Spans longer than five words are block quotations and are not counted here.

The reader is told what to do: 40 instances of *you should / can / have to / must / need to / could / get to / don't have to* — 9/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"you should go through the tedious, performatively difficult DROP process"*). Only `2026-07-11-your-risk` has none.

Questions are put to the reader rather than asked rhetorically into the air: 35 question marks — 10/10 samples, several times per piece, though 13 of the 35 are in `2026-07-13-go-meta-meta` alone (*"if you've got a chatbot that can teach my kids, why sell it to my local school district? Why not just open a school?"*).

Where a claim is softened, it is softened by a first-person marker, not an adverb: 9 instances of *I think / I believe / I strongly believe / I would guess / I'd add / I suspect* — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"I think billionaire solipsism can account for much of the malinvestment in this obvious bubble, but I don't think it's the whole story."*).

Profanity is rationed and lands on the verdict, not on the decoration: 20 instances of *shit / shits / shitty / shittiness / bullshit / fuck(s/ed/ing) / asshole(s) / piss(ed) / balls / horny*, excluding standalone URL lines (which carry these words in slugs) and excluding the coinage *dickover(s)*, which is one post's subject noun rather than an expletive — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Companies claim they need dickovers to survive. It's bullshit."*).

The floor is handed to named allies: 23 attributive clauses of the form *&lt;named person&gt; &lt;verb of saying&gt;* — 6/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"As Upton Sinclair famously quipped…"*; `2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Quiggin notes that the EU is racing to break its reliance on SWIFT"*). Counted only where the argument builds on the source: *what Uber calls "flexibility"* and *Thiel says* are excluded as targets, and *As I said* as self-reference.

The agent of a harm is a named person or firm, never only an abstraction — 10/10 samples (Uber, Tony West, Thiel, Musk, Apple, Palantir, Stephen Joyce, Keith Sonderling). No rate: the boundary of what counts as naming an opponent is not one I could apply consistently.

## 5. Figures

An analogy is opened, run for three or more consecutive paragraphs on its own terms, and only then attached to its referent — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece, 7 instances (`2026-07-22-table-flipper` spends six paragraphs on an escrow agent holding a deed before *"Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed"*; `2026-07-11-your-risk` builds the whole gig-work case out of a restaurant's tipped minimum wage). **When the claim is structural, build the analogy first and withhold the referent until the analogy has done the work.**

The images come from two wells — shared pop-culture and literary reference (Omelas, the Hitchhiker's Guide leopard, Renfield, Mr Creosote, Les Misérables, King Arthur's sword), and the body and its indignities (a lucky orifice, bladders, piss-bottles, gut flora, a dog licking its balls) — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"AI is the asbestos we're shoveling into the walls of our civilization"*). No rate: I could not draw a line around "a figure" that I could apply the same way twice.

The parenthesis is where the sneer and the caveat go: 95 parenthesised spans — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"capitalism's (alleged) defenders"*; `2026-07-10-posthuman-as-in-no-humans`: *"only to have to slow down or (gasp!) stop the machines"*).

A frame is repeated with one element swapped, three or more times — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Give Trump everything he asks for and he'll demand more. Deny Trump anything and he'll demand more. Sign a contract with Trump and he'll break it. Send Trump an invoice and he'll stiff you."*). No rate: the line between this and an ordinary three-item list is not one I could hold steady across 582 sentences.

## 6. Register range

Two of the ten turn into procedure, and the address tightens when they do — 2/10 samples. In `2026-07-21-dickovers` and `2026-07-23-drop-a-dime` second-person density roughly doubles (42.0 and 39.2 per 1,000 body words against 22.2 corpus-wide) and bare imperatives appear (*"Just click the 'Kill Sticky' bookmarklet"*).

One of the ten is about the author's own life, and there the quoting habit nearly vanishes — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears. `2026-07-14-designated-survivor` has 1 short quoted span against 7–35 in the other nine, no named-ally attributions at all, and first-person singular at 24.1 per 1,000 words against 6.2 corpus-wide.

What does not move is the machinery: across all three registers the sentence stays long and gets stopped short, the paragraph stays at two sentences, and the contraction rate holds — 10/10 samples. This is one voice changing subject, not two voices pooled; I looked for a split and did not find one.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried by a beat rather than by a hierarchy: a paragraph ends on a colon and the next line is a bare URL. There are 111 such link lines and 103 of them are immediately preceded by a colon-terminated paragraph — 10/10 samples, throughout (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"…from targeted election interference to identity theft:"*). Nothing in the corpus uses a heading, a subheading, a bulleted list or a numbered list.

The dash is always the spaced en dash, and it does the correction — 72 instances, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"Politics aren't about who rules – it's about whether we are ruled at all, or whether we are free."*). No em dash occurs anywhere in the corpus.

Concession is granted as a whole clause and then reversed: 16 concessive markers (*Sure,* / *Of course,* / *, of course,* / *Admittedly,* / *Naturally,* / *Obviously,* / *Nevertheless,* / *Granted,* / *True,*) — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Sure, forcing Uber and Lyft to extend rights and protections that every employee gets would raise their costs – but…"*). Against that, the whole 17,549 words contain 3 adverbial hedges (*arguably, perhaps, possibly, somewhat, it seems, seems to, may well, in some sense, to some extent*), in 3/10 samples.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is unfilled, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone and nothing is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

All ten samples are daily blog posts from a single 16-day window on one site — 10/10 samples. Nothing here shows how the voice behaves in a book chapter, in fiction, in a talk, or across years.

All ten address the same returning readership — 10/10 samples. Nothing shows the voice writing to a hostile reader, to a stranger, or to one named person, so the distance recorded in section 4 is the distance to a regular, not a range.

Typography is reported without an edition caveat, because `profile.json` records a living author publishing untranslated on his own site with no editor or compositor in between — but the scrape is not byte-faithful, and that limits how far the typographic counts can be pushed — 10/10 samples. Five curly apostrophes appear inside quoted matter in 3/10 samples where the surrounding prose is ASCII, and the frontmatter carries undecoded `&amp;quot;` and `&amp;#039;`. I could not rule out that the uniform spaced en dash and zero em dashes are a publishing platform's substitution rather than a keystroke.

Sample bodies range from 898 to 2,552 words — 10/10 samples. Every rate above is per 1,000 words for that reason; a draft of a different length should be measured against the rate, not against the per-piece phrase. Six observations were dropped for want of a citation I could defend, including a claim about coordination versus subordination, one about where the shortest sentence sits in a paragraph, and one about coined collective epithets that I could not partition across the ten.
