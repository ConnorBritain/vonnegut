## Finding 1 (high) — "the way water closes over a stone"

carry a concrete political consequence, never for decoration." Cited: the
punching-yourself-in-the-face figure (2026-07-22), the mare foaling a locomotive
(2026-07-10), "a demented hairball of form-filling" (2026-07-23), the asbestos figure
(2026-07-24). Where the corpus reaches for pathos "it stays literal and human."

## Finding 2 (high) — the closing image

"The piece ends on a sustained elegiac image; the corpus ends on a hard declarative claim,
every time." Ten of ten closings quoted as flat argumentative statements. Called "the
register break in its strongest form - the paragraph could be lifted into a different
document."

"(You were sold permanence, wholesale, out of a lease.)" The corpus uses parentheses for
digression, qualification or an interjected joke, "not for the sentence's best line." When
this author has an epigram "he sets it on the line in the open." Repeats at lines 15, 23, 25.

## THE SPLIT — draws 1 and 2 disagree on the same span, and both are defensible

Draw 1 named "the way water closes over a stone" as **the construction it most expected to
be absent**, went looking, and cleared it on finding "...on a shift-by-shift basis the way
restaurants get to" (2026-07-11).

Draw 2 flags the same clause at **high confidence**.

They are not making the same claim. Draw 1 matched the **syntactic frame** — a trailing
"the way X" comparison, which is attested. Draw 2 matched the **semantic function** — a
lyric image doing only mood work, which it argues is not attested.

Both readings are supportable and the evidence cited on each side is real. Per
SAMPLING-POLICY.md the split is surfaced, not resolved.

This is the clearest instance in the project of k=3 earning its cost: at k=1 either verdict
would have been the answer.
