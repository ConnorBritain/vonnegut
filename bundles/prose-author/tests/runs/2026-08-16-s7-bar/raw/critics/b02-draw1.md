All five clean, with seven constructions matched to named corpus instances.

The rhythm analysis is the best methodology in the run so far. It found a raw difference,
identified the confound, and then built a measure that survives it:

> Mean sentence length runs shorter than the corpus (18.2 vs. a 26.9-34.8 range), but that
> corpus figure is inflated by block quotes from Farrell, Sinclair, Thiel, Gruber, etc.,
> and by the long colon-terminated setups that precede each link - neither of which a
> linkless draft has. The measure that survives that confound matches: paragraphs of 3+
> sentences whose longest sentence is under 22 words run 8% in the draft (1 of 12) against
> 10% in the corpus (13 of 124), and the draft still carries five sentences of 34-46 words.
> Not a flattening I can call.

That is a critic declining to report a difference it could have reported, because it
worked out what was producing the number. It is also a caution for my own harness: any
sentence-length statistic I compute over these corpus bodies carries the same confound,
and `corpus-rates.mjs` does not correct for it.
