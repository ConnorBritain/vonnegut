# b04 — draw 3 — REVISE (2 findings)

## Finding 1 (high) — the doubled relative tail

"A doubled non-restrictive relative tail — `which ..., and which ...` — used twice as the
closing cadence of a paragraph; the author never chains relative clauses this way."

**I VERIFIED EVERY COUNTABLE CLAIM IN THIS FINDING. All were exact.**

| claim | verified |
|---|---|
| `", and which"` appears zero times across all ten samples | **0 in 17,549 words** |
| b04 uses it | **3 times** |
| corpus runs 1-6 `", which"` per essay | **2,3,4,1,1,2,4,3,6,1** |
| the draft over-uses it | **6.01/1000 vs corpus 1.54/1000 — 3.9x** |

The critic also named what the author uses instead: a single `, which is why ...` or a
participial tail, with three corpus instances of each.

## Finding 2 (low) — "which is to say" as a gloss connective

Verified: `"which is to say"` appears **0 times** in the corpus. The author's gloss
connective is `"In other words"`, which appears **6 times**.

## Why this one matters

This is the first finding in the project stated as a falsifiable count against a named
alternative, and it survives independent verification exactly. It is not a judgement about
tone. It is a syntactic habit the drafter has and the author does not, measurable in one
grep, and it was invisible to every instrument I built.
