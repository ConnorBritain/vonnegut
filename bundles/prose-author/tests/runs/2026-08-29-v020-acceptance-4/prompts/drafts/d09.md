### Observation instructions

- o01 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:second-person-family]: Direct address turns abstract systems into consequences for the reader: what a boss, platform, landlord, or government can demand of you. Use it in examples and conditional chains to make institutional power concrete, then widen back from the reader’s predicament to the general claim.
- o02 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:first-person-plural-family]: The plural shifts between an inclusive public facing shared risks and a political community capable of collective action. Use it when moving from diagnosis to common stakes or remedies; keep institutions and antagonists explicitly named so the pronoun does not blur responsibility.
- o03 [contraction-negation; section:cadence; measurement:contractions]: Contracted forms keep technical and political explanation close to speech, especially in blunt judgments, hypotheticals, and transitions such as “here’s the thing.” Use them through the argumentative narrative so complicated causal accounts remain conversational rather than acquiring bureaucratic stiffness.
- o04 [contraction-negation; section:absences; measurement:uncontracted-negatives]: Full negative forms are reserved for categorical distinctions, formal quotations, or moments when denial needs extra stress. Let contractions carry ordinary negation; expand the form only where the sentence must draw a hard conceptual boundary or echo more formal source language.
- o05 [profanity-vulgarity; section:register-range; measurement:profanity-vulgarity]: Profanity supplies compressed moral judgment, punctures euphemism, and makes an exploitative arrangement sound as indecent as the argument says it is. Place it at a reveal, punchline, quoted characterization, or decisive dismissal; do not let it replace the preceding causal explanation.
- o06 [self-reference-biography; section:register-range; measurement:first-person-singular-family]: The individual speaker appears as witness, reader, practitioner, or person revising a judgment, not as the argument’s sole authority. Use personal experience to establish why a question matters, disclose a practical stake, or mark disagreement with a source, then return promptly to the public analysis.
- o07 [questions-imperatives-vocatives; section:address; measurement:question-marks]: Questions expose a contradiction, test a sales pitch on its own terms, or introduce the practical problem the next passage answers. Direct them toward claims and systems rather than soliciting audience participation; after asking, pursue the implications through examples until the premise fails.
- o08 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:round-parenthetical-spans]: Parentheses carry definitions, sardonic precision, miniature counterarguments, and technical qualifications without surrendering the main sentence’s momentum. Use them for compact side-information whose removal leaves the claim intact, or for a quick comic twist that sharpens rather than diverts the analysis.
- o09 [interruption-punctuation; section:absences; measurement:em-dashes]: Do not rely on the em dash for interruption. Put substantial asides in parentheses and use the en dash for pivots, apposition, and emphatic qualification; this keeps interruptions visibly assigned a function instead of making one mark perform every kind of turn.
- o10 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:en-dashes]: The en dash creates forceful pivots inside long explanations: it can restate an abstraction concretely, insert a corrective qualification, or deliver the consequence after a buildup. Use it where the second phrase revises or intensifies the first, while parentheses handle genuinely detachable asides.
- o11 [qualification-hedging; section:address]: Qualification usually concedes a limited truth before narrowing its reach: a proposal may be plausible, a risk real, or an opponent technically correct, “but” the distributional or practical consequence changes the verdict. Place concessions immediately before the mechanism that defeats the broader claim.
- o12 [opponents-allies-sources; section:register-range]: Sources enter by name with a compact credential or memorable proposition, after which the prose translates, tests, or extends their insight. Opponents are named as institutions, executives, doctrines, or quoted sales pitches. Credit allies before building outward; quote adversaries before exposing the mechanism their wording conceals.
- o13 [figures-analogy; section:figures]: Analogies draw from markets, workplaces, scams, machinery, bureaucracy, disease, and familiar stories. They begin as a vivid equivalence, then extend across several matching parts until an abstract power relation becomes operationally obvious. Land the comparison near the explanatory center or final synthesis, not as ornamental scene-setting.
- o14 [openings-endings-closure; section:closings]: Openings state a charged proposition, anomaly, quotation, or apparently reasonable theory, then reveal the contradiction that will organize the piece. Paragraphs often end on a consequence, reversal, or blunt compression. Close by returning to the governing terms and sharpening them into a verdict, remedy, or collective obligation.
