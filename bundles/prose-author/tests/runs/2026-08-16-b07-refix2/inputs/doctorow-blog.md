# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

Ten usable samples, all with provenance; nothing excluded. `voice.md` is unfilled, so everything below comes from the corpus.

**Counting scope, stated once.** All counts are over the essay body of all ten samples — the text between the first `(permalink)` line and the next section-title `(permalink)` line — minus bare-URL lines and the trailing `(Image: …)` credits. That is 17,417 words. Counts include words quoted from other writers unless an observation says otherwise. Rates are that count divided by 17,417, times a thousand.

## 1. Cadence

Sentences are long and the length is stable: mean 23.8 words, median 23, and per-sample means clustered between 21.8 and 26.5. The long sentence is the default vehicle — one that keeps adding qualifying clauses rather than starting a new sentence. Sentences over 40 words: **72 instances, 4.13 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-11`: *"Uber exploits its information asymmetry to publish only the numerator (the amount a driver makes when a passenger is in the car) while hiding the denominator (how many hours it takes for Uber to put a passenger in that car)"*).

The short sentence is a paragraph *opener*, not a paragraph closer. Sentences of six words or fewer: **50 instances, 2.87 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-23`: *"DROP didn't get the memo."*). Twenty of those fifty are the first sentence of their paragraph. Only 24% of short sentences end their paragraph, against a 40.5% baseline for all 733 sentences — so if you want the flat verdict, put it at the top of the paragraph and unpack it underneath, not at the bottom as a punch.

Paragraphs end on a colon that hands off to a source. Counting paragraphs whose last character is `:` — **112 instances, 6.43 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-24`: *"Austerity is fascism's best recruiting tool:"*). 103 of the 112 are followed by a bare link.

Adjacent sentences repeat their first two words. Counting only pairs of consecutive sentences inside one paragraph whose first two words match, case-insensitively: **18 instances, 1.03 per 1,000 words — 7/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-09`: *"If property rights are human rights, I can leave an apartment building empty while you freeze to death on its sidewalk."*).

Sentence-initial *But*: **36 instances, 2.07 per 1,000 words — 9/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-21`: *"But it doesn't have to be this way."*).

The interrupting dash. Counting the en dash `–` as a character: **72 instances, 4.13 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-09`: *"they have mistaken a collection of cultural signifiers – pronouns, MMA, brands of beer – for politics"*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three kinds, and I checked all ten. Six open on a flat declarative verdict on the subject inside the first clause, before any evidence — **6/10 samples** (`2026-07-23`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*). Openings and closings are one-per-piece, so their count and their support are the same number and a separate rate would only restate it.

Three open presentatively and hand immediately to somebody else's sentence — **3/10 samples** (`2026-07-11`: *"Here's an irony: the 'gig economy' is a statistical black hole."*).

One opens on an epigraph the writer then narrates his own history with — **1/10 samples, the only place this appears** (`2026-07-13`: *"I often wonder what the Vintners buy/One half so precious as the Goods they sell"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The last paragraph takes the term the piece has been contesting and turns it back on whoever coined it — **6/10 samples** (`2026-07-16`: *"But the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires and convinces them that they are above any discipline or rules."*). No rate: I cannot write a rule for "the term the piece has been contesting" that a stranger reproduces from the text alone. Of the other four, `2026-07-09` closes on an antithesis pair, `2026-07-14` on a bare judging adjective, `2026-07-22` on a colon handing to a link, and `2026-07-23` on a restated demand.

The closer is not a short punch. Final paragraphs run 29 to 86 words, mean 55.9, and every one is more than one sentence — **10/10 samples**.

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The second person carries the argument. Counting `you`, `your`, `yours`, `yourself`, `yourselves`, including the `you` inside `you're`: **390 instances, 22.39 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-21`: *"you're not decreasing the amount of technological confusion they're likely to experience in a day – you're vastly increasing the amount of danger they face"*). The reader is put inside the example as the person it happens to.

Alongside it, a *we* that includes the reader — counting `we`, `us`, `our`, `ours`, `ourselves`: **163 instances, 9.36 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-23`: *"In a state of nature, murder exists. We, as a society, have decided this is bad."*). Both stances run at once: *you* for the thing being done to you, *we* for what is to be done about it.

The writer is present as a person with a biography, not only as an arguer. Counting `I`, `me`, `my`, `mine`, `myself`, **excluding** tokens inside words quoted from another named speaker or writer but including the writer's own invented dialogue: **100 instances, 5.74 per 1,000 words — 9/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-14`: *"I'm 55."*). The facts offered are specific: an age, an illness, a child, a dead friend.

Contraction is near-total. Counting `n't`, `'re`, `'ve`, `'ll`, `'m`, `'d` after a pronoun, and `'s` only after the closed list *it, that, there, here, what, who, he, she, let, how, where, this, one, everything, nothing, something, someone, everyone, anyone* — never as a possessive: **299 instances, 17.17 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-24`: *"It's not exceptionally wicked. It's not exceptionally good."*).

The opponent's own word is held in quotation marks and then dismantled. Counting double-quoted spans of four words or fewer: **152 instances, 8.73 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-11`: *"what Uber calls 'flexibility' is really a way for the company to offload its operational risks onto its drivers"*). `2026-07-14` is the outlier with one.

Allies are named and given the floor. Counting a personal proper name immediately governing a finite speech-or-writing verb from the list *writes, wrote, says, said, notes, argues, points out, puts it, quipped, told, calls, cites, would call, likes to say*: **26 instances, 1.49 per 1,000 words — 6/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-16`: *"As Upton Sinclair famously quipped"*).

Profanity is rationed, not decorative. Counting the list *shit, shits, shitty, shittiness, bullshit, fuck, fucking, fucked, asshole, assholes, piss, pissed, damn, damned, crap, horny*, and excluding *dickover*, which is a coined term under discussion in `2026-07-21` rather than an oath: **20 instances, 1.15 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece** (`2026-07-23`: *"Data brokers spy on us for the same reason your dog licks its balls"*). Two per piece, and each lands at the moment of maximum contempt.

Questions are asked of the reader and answered on the spot. Counting `?` characters: **35 instances, 2.01 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-23`: *"Why do data brokers exist? Because we let them."*). The answer is usually shorter than the question.

The parenthetical aside. Counting any round-bracketed span of 1–120 characters, glosses and numerals included: **88 instances, 5.05 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-14`: *"America's (very, very weird) political system"*).

## 5. Figures

The characteristic image is an ordinary small transaction stood up as a working model of the large one, then run for several paragraphs and made to do the argument's labour — the escrow agent in `2026-07-22` (*"Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed, then announces he did it because the seller was a fentanyl dealer and the buyer was a lizard-person"*), the restaurateur and the tipped minimum wage in `2026-07-11`, the baker's sawdust loaf in `2026-07-16`. When the image is borrowed from a book, a film or a joke, the owner is named — **10/10 samples**. No rate: I cannot state a rule for where a figure starts and stops that a stranger would apply the same way.

The figures are assembled out of hyphenated compounds and coinages, and that unit *is* countable. Counting tokens matching lowercase-hyphen-lowercase, two or more letters each side: **168 instances, 9.65 per 1,000 words — 10/10 samples, throughout** (`2026-07-14`: *"chud-dominated"*; `2026-07-13`: *"broccoli-haired brownshirts"*). This is not a count of figures. It is a count of the unit they are built from, under the rule just stated — **use hyphenated compounds at about this rate.**

A second countable component: the body and its excretions supply the vocabulary of contempt. Counting the list *orifice, orifices, piss, piss-bottles, pee, bladder, bladders, balls, vagina, vaginas, asshole, assholes, kidney, kidneys, gut, guts, flora, throat, stomach, blood, bodies, body*: **20 instances, 1.15 per 1,000 words — 7/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-09`: *"if you emerged from a lucky orifice"*). Use that vocabulary at about this rate.

## 6. Register range

The register moves with what the piece is for, and the movement is in the pronouns, not the sentences. Second-person density runs from 11.4 per 1,000 words in the pure polemic (`2026-07-16`) to 42.4 in the how-to (`2026-07-21`); first-person density runs from zero (`2026-07-16`) to 24.3 in the piece that turns personal (`2026-07-14`). What comes with the how-to register is the bare imperative — counting sentence-initial imperatives from the closed list *Just, Take, Stop, Select, Note, Think, Consider, Imagine, Try, Click, Install, Look, Switch, Use, Give, Deny, Sign, Send, Ask, Remember, Load, Hover, Move, Read*, excluding *Just as*: **19 instances, 1.09 per 1,000 words — 7/10 samples, several times per piece** (`2026-07-21`: *"Stop using Chrome, switch to Firefox"*), 13 of the 19 concentrated in two pieces. Sentence length and contraction rate do not move with any of this.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried inside the prose — by the colon-and-link handoff above, and by the one-sentence pivot paragraph that turns the piece. Counting paragraphs of a single sentence, twelve words or fewer: **13 instances, 0.75 per 1,000 words — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece** (`2026-07-23`: *"DROP didn't get the memo."*). No sample uses a heading, a bulleted list, or a numbered list — **10/10 samples**.

Judgements arrive as bare copular assertions with the verdict in the predicate — **10/10 samples** (`2026-07-22`: *"Sovereign AI is, frankly, nonsense."*). No rate: I cannot bound "a judgement" reproducibly. Against 17,417 words there is no *perhaps*, no *arguably*, no *I suspect*, and *probably* twice; the hedge, when there is one, is a first-person frame (*I think*, six instances) rather than an adverb.

Nothing signposts itself as a summary: across 17,417 words there are zero instances of *in conclusion, to sum up, overall, in summary, to conclude, all in all, in short* — **10/10 samples**. The work a summary would do is done instead by the closing return of the contested term described in section 3.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is present but unfilled, so there is no authorial account of intent to corroborate or contradict — everything here is derived from the corpus alone — **10/10 samples**.

All ten samples are the same medium, the same publication, and a sixteen-day window in July 2026, and all ten are argumentative posts addressed to a standing readership. Nothing here shows the voice in fiction, in correspondence, in a talk, at any other length, or aimed at a reader who does not already agree — **10/10 samples**.

These files are HTML scrapes, not manuscripts: 5 curly apostrophes against 457 straight ones, zero em dashes against 72 en dashes, and entities left in the frontmatter. The punctuation counts in sections 1 and 4 are therefore counts of what the scraper emitted; the profile.json states no editor or translator sits between writer and page, so I have not caveated them further, but a drafter should know they are the one class of finding here that a pipeline could have touched — **10/10 samples**. The grammatical and structural findings are unaffected.

I dropped 8 observations for want of evidence or of a reproducible counting rule.
