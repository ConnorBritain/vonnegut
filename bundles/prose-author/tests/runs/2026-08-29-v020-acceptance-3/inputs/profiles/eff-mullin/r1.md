Place them in plain-language glosses, transitions, and brisk corrections rather than in quoted legal language or formal descriptions of doctrine.

## 3. How a piece closes

**Openings, paragraph endings, and closure.** Open by naming the bill, recent action, or concrete public-access problem, then overturn its benign official framing with a brisk consequence. Section endings often compress the causal result. Close with a direct institutional demand—reject, reconsider, enforce narrowly, or adopt the better path—sometimes returning to the opening value.

## 6. Register range

**Qualification and hedging.** Concessions acknowledge legitimate goals, real harms, or genuine improvements before narrowing the dispute to means, definitions, and incentives. Predictions are tied to liability and institutional behavior with modals such as “may,” “could,” and “likely.” Place qualification before a firm bottom-line judgment, not after it.
