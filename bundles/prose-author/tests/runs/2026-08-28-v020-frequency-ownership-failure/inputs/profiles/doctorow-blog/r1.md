## 2. How a piece opens

**Openings, paragraph endings, and closure.** Pieces open in one of three shapes: an ironic framing (‘Here’s an irony’ — 2026-07-11-your-risk.txt; ‘Here’s a sentence that stopped me in my tracks’ — 2026-07-22-table-flipper.txt); a term introduced and defined (‘One of 2026’s better tech-related coinages is “dickover”’ — 2026-07-21-dickovers.txt); or a theoretical proposition immediately put under pressure (‘The theory of markets goes like this’ — 2026-07-16-lucky-orifices.txt). None begin with the central claim; all begin with the frame that makes the claim legible.
_Evidence: 10/10 samples; once or twice per piece. Representative locked source: `2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt`._

## 3. How a piece closes

**Openings, paragraph endings, and closure.** Closings return to the opening frame or compress the argument’s stakes into a single portable formulation. ‘What they call “theft” is just self-defense’ (2026-07-21-dickovers.txt). ‘Politics aren’t about who rules — it’s about whether we are ruled at all, or whether we are free’ (2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt). The final paragraph introduces no new evidence; it turns the accumulated argument into a sentence the reader can carry away. End with the sharpest version of the thesis, not a summary of the path taken.
_Evidence: 8/10 samples; once or twice per piece. Representative locked source: `2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt`._

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

**Person, number, and reader stance.** The prose addresses the reader as ‘you’ in every piece — not the instructional ‘you’ of a manual, but the ‘you’ of political solidarity. The reader is placed inside the situation being analysed: ‘it is the company’s actions, not the driver’s, that determine whether that driver gets a job’ (2026-07-11-your-risk.txt). Write ‘you’ when the reader is the person on whom the described system operates.

Count: 385 instances; 21.94 per 1,000 words. Representative locked source: `2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt`._

**Person, number, and reader stance.** ‘We’ appears alongside ‘you’ as a co-sufferer or co-citizen pronoun: ‘We got rid of pop-ups by installing pop-up blockers’ (2026-07-21-dickovers.txt) places writer and reader together in historical action. Use ‘we’ when the claim is collective — something done or endured together — and ‘you’ when the cost falls on the reader individually. These two stances alternate within single pieces without friction.

**Questions, imperatives, and vocatives.** Rhetorical questions drive the argument forward and are answered in the next sentence or paragraph. ‘Why would they operate a hospital if they could go meta and sell the doctorbots to the MBAs running the hospital?’ (2026-07-13-go-meta-meta.txt). The question is never left open; it frames an answer the prose has already prepared. Pose the question, then deliver the answer in the sentence that follows it.

**Named opponents, allies, and sources.** Named sources are handed the floor briefly, then extended or corrected: ‘As Weil writes,’ ‘As Quiggin writes,’ ‘As Tim O’Reilly writes’ — the attribution opens the engagement (2026-07-11-your-risk.txt, 2026-07-22-table-flipper.txt, 2026-07-16-lucky-orifices.txt). Opponents are also named directly: Trump, Uber, Thiel. Front-load the name when citing an ally; name the opponent before characterising their position, not after it.

**Self-reference and biography.** The writer enters as a named individual with history: age, illness, named friends, a daughter’s age. ‘My doctor just informed me that I am cancer free, following excision, radiotherapy and immunotherapy’ (2026-07-14-designated-survivor.txt). These biographical facts are not incidental — they anchor abstract actuarial or strategic claims in personal stakes. Place the biographical detail before the abstract claim it grounds, not as an afterthought.

## 5. Figures

**Figures and analogy vocabulary.** Analogies are built from concrete, everyday transactions — restaurants, escrow agents, markets, bears — to explain abstract economic or political structures. ‘Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed, then announces he did it because the seller was a fentanyl dealer’ (2026-07-22-table-flipper.txt). The analogy is extended until it collapses under the target’s own absurdity. Choose a familiar transaction, run it until it exposes the logic as incoherent.
_Evidence: 6/10 samples; several times per piece. Representative locked source: `2026-07-10-posthuman-as-in-no-humans.txt`._

## 6. Register range

**Qualification and hedging.** When uncertainty is acknowledged, it arrives as a first-person marker rather than a hedging adverb: ‘I think the only reason this question doesn’t come up more frequently’ (2026-07-13-go-meta-meta.txt), ‘I think there’s a whole cohort of investors’ (2026-07-24-supplemental-income.txt). Most claims land without any qualification at all. If you need to hedge, write ‘I think’ or ‘I’d add’; do not reach for ‘perhaps,’ ‘arguably,’ or ‘it could be said.’
_Evidence: 5/10 samples; once or twice per piece. Representative locked source: `2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt`._

**Profanity and vulgarity.** Profanity appears in every piece but is rationed, arriving at the moment of maximum contempt or absurdity: ‘tell the cartel to fuck off’ (2026-07-09-wilhoitian.txt), ‘holy shit, is Uber’s investor relations site seriously serving ASPX pages’ (2026-07-11-your-risk.txt). The expletive marks a rupture in register that signals genuine disgust rather than rhetorical heat. Spend it once; a second use in a piece should feel more earned than the first.

## 7. What the corpus never does

**Contraction and negation.** Uncontracted negation — ‘do not,’ ‘cannot,’ ‘will not’ — is near-absent across the corpus.
