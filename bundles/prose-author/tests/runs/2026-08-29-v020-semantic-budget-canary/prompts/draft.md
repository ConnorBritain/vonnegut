- o01 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:second-person-family]: Direct address makes abstract systems personally legible by placing the reader inside a transaction, workplace, interface, or threat model. - o03 [contraction-negation; section:cadence; measurement:contractions]: Contractions keep explanation conversational even when the subject is technical, legal, or economic. - o04 [contraction-negation; section:absences; measurement:uncontracted-negatives]: Full negative forms yield to contractions as the ordinary vehicle for denial and rebuttal. - o05 [profanity-vulgarity; section:register-range; measurement:profanity-vulgarity]: Profanity acts as a pressure spike: it punctures euphemism, translates an official rationale into blunt consequences, or supplies a comic insult after the mechanism has been established. - o06 [self-reference-biography; section:register-range; measurement:first-person-singular-family]: The individual speaker appears as witness, reader, practitioner, or participant rather than as the argument’s sole subject. - o07 [questions-imperatives-vocatives; section:address; measurement:question-marks]: Questions usually structure the argument rather than solicit information. They expose a contradiction, voice the obvious challenge to a sales pitch, or launch a sequence whose answers tighten the case. Pose them near a hinge, then answer directly through examples, consequences, or a compact reductio.
- o08 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:round-parenthetical-spans]: Parentheses carry quick definitions, sardonic corrections, examples, acronyms, and intensifying asides without stopping the main claim. Use them inside an already intelligible sentence to add a second argumentative layer; the parenthetical should sharpen the reader’s interpretation rather than hold indispensable logic.
- o09 [interruption-punctuation; section:absences; measurement:em-dashes]: Interruption does not rely on the em dash; en dashes and parentheses perform the visible work of pivots, qualifications, and embedded commentary. Keep em dashes out of ordinary drafting, choosing parentheses for detachable asides and spaced en dashes for forceful turns within a sentence.
- o10 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:en-dashes]: Spaced en dashes create abrupt pivots from premise to consequence, insert a pointed gloss, or suspend a sentence before its sharper restatement. Use them where the second unit changes the argumentative pressure or exposes what an official phrase conceals, not merely to decorate a clause boundary.
- o11 [qualification-hedging; section:register-range]: Qualification distinguishes factual limits from rhetorical confidence: concessions such as “of course,” “while,” and “even if” briefly grant the strongest plausible objection before narrowing or overturning it. - o13 [figures-analogy; section:figures]: Analogies draw from markets, workplaces, machinery, scams, bodily risk, and familiar stories. A comparison is often extended across several clauses until each part maps onto the power relation under analysis. Introduce it after the mechanism, use it to expose absurdity, then return explicitly to the claim.
- o14 [openings-endings-closure; section:closings]: Openings commonly present a paradox, definition, arresting quotation, or compressed thesis, then unpack the hidden mechanism. Paragraphs land on a consequence, inversion, or blunt reformulation. Closings return to the central phrase or analogy and convert it into a verdict, obligation, or practical demand.
