## 6. Register range

**Profanity and vulgarity.** Profanity supplies a sharp verdict after evidence has exposed deception, predation, incompetence, or absurdity. It also animates quoted attitudes and deliberately coarse comparisons. Place it at a pressure point or punchline rather than using it as ambient intensification, and keep the surrounding explanation analytically precise.

**Self-reference and biography.** The singular speaker appears as witness, reader, practitioner, or arguer: recalling an encounter, disclosing a relevant stake, naming a judgment, or marking disagreement with a source. Use personal material to establish why an example matters or to pivot the analysis, not as detached autobiography.

**Qualification and hedging.** Qualification distinguishes factual limits from argumentative confidence. Concessions commonly begin with forms such as “of course,” “sure,” or “admittedly,” grant a narrow point, and then show why it does not rescue the larger claim. Use conditional chains and explicit scope limits before delivering the firmer consequence.
