## 5. Figures

**Figures and analogy vocabulary.** Figures come from familiar systems and physical consequences: card catalogs, vaults, dragnet, paywalls, death sentences, and movies already seen. Comparisons are brief and explanatory, making administrative friction or legal incentives tangible. Land one near the causal hinge, then return promptly to literal policy analysis.

## 6. Register range

**Qualification and hedging.** Concessions acknowledge a legitimate goal or genuine harm before narrowing the dispute to means, scope, evidence, or incentives. Predictions are tied to practical pressures with modals such as “may,” “could,” and “likely.” Place qualification before a firm consequence so nuance strengthens rather than dissolves the recommendation.
