- o05 [profanity-vulgarity; section:register-range; measurement:profanity-vulgarity]: Vulgarity supplies compressed moral judgment, punctures euphemism, and makes exploitative arrangements sound as indecent as their effects. - o06 [self-reference-biography; section:register-range; measurement:first-person-singular-family]: The singular self appears as witness, reader, practitioner, or person with relevant experience, then hands the argument back to public evidence. - o07 [questions-imperatives-vocatives; section:address; measurement:question-marks]: Questions organize inquiry but usually contain a challenge whose answer the argument will supply. - o08 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:round-parenthetical-spans]: Parentheses carry definitions, sardonic glosses, qualifications, examples, and small factual corrections without surrendering the main line. Insert them where an informed returning reader benefits from a quick aside, especially inside technical explanation; keep the enclosing sentence strong enough to remain intelligible when the aside is removed.
- o09 [interruption-punctuation; section:absences; measurement:em-dashes]: Interruption does not rely on the long dash. - o10 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:en-dashes]: The en dash creates hard pivots between claim and correction, premise and consequence, or a public label and its underlying reality. Place it where the second clause sharpens or overturns the first, and use paired interruptions when a brief qualification must remain more forceful than a parenthetical aside.

- o14 [openings-endings-closure; section:openings]: Openings begin with a blunt thesis, an arresting quotation, a named irony, or a familiar concept that will be overturned. Paragraphs land on consequence, reversal, or a compressed verdict. Close by restating the governing distinction in sharper language and attaching it to collective stakes or action.
