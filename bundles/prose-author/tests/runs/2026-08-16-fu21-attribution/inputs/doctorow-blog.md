# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

## 1. Cadence

Sentences run long by default: 709 sentences across the ten bodies average 24.6 words, and 107 of them run 40 words or longer — 10/10 samples, several times per piece. The long ones accumulate by coordination and apposition rather than subordination, adding another item to a list that is already complete (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"They should understand that capitalists hate capitalism, that every pirate yearns to be an admiral."*).

The accumulation is stopped by a verdict sentence of six words or fewer — 60 such sentences, 9/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"It's bullshit."*; `2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"DROP didn't get the memo."*). This is the reproducible half: when a sentence has run long and explanatory, make the next one under six words and let it deliver the judgement the long one only set up.

The mid-sentence colon is the workhorse joint — 188 instances, 10/10 samples, throughout. It sets up a definition, a list, or a punchline, and the payload after it is usually the shorter half (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Here's an irony: the 'gig economy' is a statistical black hole."*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, not one. **A flat declarative verdict, before any evidence** — 4/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*; `2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"As a technology, AI isn't exceptional."*).

**A deictic pointer — "Here's a/an X:" — that hands straight to a quote or fact** — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Here's a sentence that stopped me in my tracks last week:"*).

**Someone else's words or someone else's position first, in their own terms** — 4/10 samples (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta` opens on a Rubáiyát couplet; `2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"The theory of markets goes like this:"*, then two paragraphs stating the opposing case sympathetically before turning on it).

## 3. How a piece closes

The last paragraph takes the piece's own key term — usually a phrase borrowed from the opposition — and re-points it — 8/10 samples (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"they value their flexibility, which arises out of the constraints (technical, legal) they impose on us"*; `2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires"*). To do it: pick the word the other side chose, and make the last sentence mean it their way and yours at once.

Two pieces end not on a sentence but on a colon and a bare link — 2/10 samples, the only place this happens (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"the most exciting investment hypothesis is that 'hell is other people':"*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The reader is *you*, constantly — 395 instances of *you/your/you're/you've*, 10/10 samples, throughout. Often the *you* is put inside the argument as its victim or its beneficiary (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Every piece of your information that a data broker possesses exposes you to the risk of being victimized by a criminal."*).

Contractions are the default, not a relaxation: 291 contracted forms against 9 uncontracted *do not / is not / cannot* forms — 10/10 samples, throughout.

There is also a *we/us/our* that includes the reader, and it carries the moral claims specifically — 127 instances, 10/10 samples, several times per piece, but very unevenly (36 in `2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`, 3 in `2026-07-14-designated-survivor`). Both stances are live: *you* for what is being done to the reader, *we* for what is to be done about it (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"AI is the asbestos we're shoveling into the walls of our civilization"*).

Direct questions are put to the reader and then answered — 38 question marks, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"if you've truly incubated a foetal demiurge in your 'AI lab,' why are you offering to sell it to me?"*).

Profanity is present but rationed to the moment of maximum contempt, never used as decoration — 20 instances, 9/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"That's the whole fucking point of this exercise"*).

The opposition is named, individually and by institution — Uber, Thiel, Musk, Trump, Carney, Tony West, the CFPB — rather than referred to as an abstract tendency: 10/10 samples (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Tony West isn't lying when he says Uber values flexibility"*).

Allies are given the floor by name and built on, with an attributive clause carrying the handover — 8/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"As Quiggin writes, Europe and Ukraine have largely given up on US military protection"*; `2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"what Douglas Rushkoff calls the 'go meta' economy"*). The pattern is: name, attribute, extend, and once — in `2026-07-22-table-flipper` — disagree in the same voice (*"there's one area where I think Quiggin's got it wrong"*).

## 5. Figures

Images are made of bodies, food, animals, domestic objects and money — 10/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers spy on us for the same reason your dog licks its balls"*; `2026-07-10-posthuman-as-in-no-humans`: *"if we breed horses to run ever faster, one of our mares will foal a locomotive"*; `2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"like punching yourself in the face as hard as you can in the hopes that the downstairs neighbour says 'ouch'"*). They run one clause and are not reopened.

Alongside those, each piece carries one analogy sustained across several paragraphs, used as the actual argument rather than as ornament — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece: the escrow agent in `2026-07-22-table-flipper`, the tipped-minimum-wage restaurant in `2026-07-11-your-risk`, Puritan divine providence in `2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`, murder offsets in `2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`.

The furniture is borrowed from popular and literary culture and assumed shared, never glossed — 8/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"a locked filing cabinet stuck in a disused lavatory with a sign on the door saying 'Beware of the Leopard'"*).

## 6. Register range

The voice moves toward memoir when the argument needs a personal stake, and the move is marked by first-person singular density — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"I'm 55. 20 years ago, it was unusual for just one of my peers to die in a given year; now I lose a couple every year."*).

It moves toward second-person procedural instruction, with imperatives and literal keystrokes — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Just hit ctrl-alt-r and see if the whole article appears."*).

What does not move across those shifts is the address: contraction and direct *you* hold at the same level in the memoir piece, the how-to piece and the political-economy pieces — 10/10 samples.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried by the link: a paragraph ends on a colon and the evidence follows as a bare URL on its own line — 103 instances, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"'Horseshoe theory' … is bullshit:"* followed by the link). Nothing in the corpus breaks a piece up with a heading, a bulleted list, or a numbered list — zero in all ten.

Verdicts arrive unhedged and in the indicative; the softeners that exist are first-person markers (*I think*, *I'd add*) rather than adverbial hedges — 19 hedges total, 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"I think there's a whole cohort of investors who don't believe in AI"*). No claim in the corpus is softened by *arguably*, *it could be argued*, or *to some extent*.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is present but unfilled, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone — 10/10 samples.

All ten samples are self-published posts from one blog inside a 16-day window on one subject cluster (tech, political economy), so nothing here shows the voice at another length, in another medium, or on another subject — 10/10 samples.

All ten address a returning readership presumed to share the politics; nothing shows how the address changes for a hostile or unfamiliar reader — 10/10 samples.

Bodies run 898–2,552 words. Nothing shows what the opening and closing shapes do at, say, 400 words, and the frequencies above are per-piece rates measured on pieces of that size — 10/10 samples.
