# PI-02 · FU-21 — whose words carry the argument (2026-08-16)

**Result: 3 of 3 renders record the habit, against 1 of 4 before. The drafter now sees it
and declines it honestly on a topic where it cannot be applied — a silent gap became a
disclosed omission.**

---

## 1. The gap

b04 failed the S6 re-run 3/3 on *"never routes an argument through a named person"*, two
draws at high confidence, one listing the corpus habit exhaustively: *"As Weil writes," "As
Quiggin notes," "As Upton Sinclair famously quipped," "what Douglas Rushkoff calls."*

FU-20's measurement showed this was **not** a regression from the rate rule. The habit
appeared in 1 of 4 renders overall — an unreliable catch, predating FU-19.

The register checklist covered person, contraction, hedging, naming the opposition,
profanity and self-reference. **It had nothing about whose words carry the argument.** And
the opposition row actively masked the gap: a renderer that records "the opposition is
named" has covered proper nouns without covering attribution.

## 2. The fix

One checklist row:

> **whose words carry the argument** — Does the writer argue alone, or hand the floor to
> named others — *as X writes*, *what Y calls*, *Z's term for* — and build on, extend or
> correct them? This is about **allies and sources**, not targets: a writer can name
> everyone they attack and still never cite anyone they agree with. Look for the
> attributive clause, not the proper noun.

The allies-vs-targets sentence is doing the work. The distinction is what four earlier
renders collapsed.

## 3. Result — 3 of 3

| draw | records it? | form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **yes** | *"The floor is handed to a named ally with an attributive tag... — 30 tags, 7/10 samples"* — rated |
| 2 | **yes** | *"Named others are handed the floor by an attributive clause and then built on — 22 instances, 7/10 samples"* — rated |
| 3 | **yes** | *"Allies are given the floor by name and built on, with an attributive clause carrying the handover — 8/10 samples"* — unrated |

All three cite the corpus instances. All three keep it **separate** from the
opposition-naming observation in the same section, which is exactly the distinction the
checklist row added. Ship criterion part 1: **met** (≥ 2 of 3).

Draws 1 and 2 found it countable and rated it; draw 3 recorded it unrated. Both are correct
under the prompt, which requires a rate only where instances can be enumerated.

## 4. Part 2 — a better outcome than "the finding stops recurring"

b04 re-drafted against a profile carrying the habit. Its `omitted` list:

> *"naming an ally and handing over with an attributive clause before extending them —
> would require a real quotation from a real writer, which I cannot verify"*

**The drafter saw the habit, tried to apply it, and refused rather than fabricate an
attribution.** That is the anti-fabrication rule and the disclosure contract both working,
on a topic where the drafter holds no verified quotations.

So the state has genuinely changed: before, the profile did not record the habit and the
draft was silently missing it. Now the profile records it, the drafter reaches for it, and
the shortfall is **on the record in the artefact** where a publisher can see it.

**k=3 critique run 2026-08-16. b04 converts.**

| | findings | mean | majority | bar |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| b04 pre-FU-21 (S6 re-run) | 1, 1, 3 | 1.67 | REVISE | **fail** |
| b04 post-FU-21 | 2, 0, 0 | **0.67** | CLEAN | **pass** |

**The named-source finding did not recur on any draw.** It was the unanimous cause of the
original failure and it is gone. Draw 3 considered attribution explicitly and dropped it as
non-evidence, having found a corpus counter-example itself: *"posthuman-as-in-no-humans, the
closest genre match, likewise attributes nothing to a named external thinker, so absence of
attribution is not a deviation from this author."*

That is a better outcome than the criterion asked for. The habit is now in the profile, the
drafter reaches for it, discloses when it cannot honestly apply it — and the critic, reading
the corpus directly, agrees that on this genre the absence is not a defect.

Draw 1's two findings are new and unrelated to attribution (elegiac figuration; no
within-piece register break). The second is filed as FU-22 and tempered there, because draws
2 and 3 cleared that category.

## 5. Incidental — the renderer rejected FU-14's original hypothesis

Draw 1 lists observations it formed and dropped for want of support. Among them:

> *that paragraphs end on their shortest sentence*

That is precisely the belief FU-14's three failed fixes were built on. A renderer reading
the corpus directly declined to record it — the corpus ends paragraphs **longer** than it
writes generally.

## 6. Honest limits

- **k=3 on one corpus.** The habit is well-attested in this corpus; a corpus where it is
  rarer would test the checklist row harder.
- **Part 2 is one draft, uncritiqued.** The disclosure is the result; the critic's response
  to it is unmeasured.
- **The checklist grows by accretion.** This is the second row added after a failure found
  it missing. That is a working process and also an admission that the list is not derived
  from anything — it is what has been noticed so far.
