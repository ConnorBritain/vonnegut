The ad-tech industry has a miracle to sell you: targeting that knows more and sees less. Every time a privacy law lands or a browser switches off third-party cookies, the trade press fills up with the same promise, that whatever replaces the cookie will be sharper than the thing it replaced *and* gentler at the same time, because the data stays on your device, or gets blurred with noise, or gets folded into a cohort of thousands, or never leaves the "clean room". It's a good pitch. It's made in good faith by a lot of engineers who believe it.

So start with a tailor, because a tailor is this business run honestly. You walk into a shop with a counter and a till, you stand still while a man runs a tape around your chest and your inseam and the shoulder that sits lower than the other one, and three weeks later you collect a jacket that fits you and fits nobody else on earth. Here's his whole trade in one line: the fit *is* the measuring. There's no separate ingredient called "fit" that he could have stirred in at the end if only he'd had more of it — the jacket hangs right exactly to the degree that the tape told him the truth about your body. Ask him to make it fit better and you're asking him to measure more. Ask him to measure less and you're asking for something off a rack.

That's the entire argument, and the industry has spent years insisting it doesn't apply to them. Accuracy in targeting isn't a property that lives in the model or the auction or the "signal" — it's a count of how much the system knows about you that you never chose to tell it. An ad that's better aimed is an ad that has narrowed you down further, and being narrowed down is what the invasion *is*. These aren't two dials with a tragic tradeoff between them that some future cleverness will dissolve: it's one dial with two labels. What the industry is selling us is the map of the territory drawn at 1:1 scale, with a promise that it folds up small enough for your pocket.

Every dodge is technically interesting and not one of them changes the arithmetic. On-device processing means the raw data stays on your phone, which sounds wonderful right up until you notice that the phone then reports out the conclusion it drew from that data, and the conclusion was always the invasive part: the advertiser never wanted your location history, it wanted to know you're pregnant, or job-hunting, or drinking again. Adding statistical noise makes the targeting worse on purpose, which isn't a flaw in the method, it's the method, and the knob you tune to decide how much noise to add is a knob labelled "how much accuracy would you like back". Cohorts hide you in a crowd, and a crowd small enough for a buyer to pay for is a crowd you can be picked out of.

Google's Topics API is the tell. It replaced FLoC with a shorter, blunter list of interest labels, and it really is less invasive than the third-party cookie it was built to retire — which is precisely why the buy side has been complaining about it since the day it shipped: it tells them less. Less is the improvement. Less is also unsellable to an industry whose entire business was built on more, which is why every "privacy-preserving" scheme that survives contact with a media buyer comes back a little more accurate than the one before it.

So the phrase is bullshit in the strict sense: it isn't a lie about the technology, it's a lie about the word. The privacy on offer is the privacy of a waiting room where everyone already knows what's wrong with your bowels and has agreed not to say it out loud. That isn't privacy. That's discretion, and discretion is a courtesy that the file on you does not extend.

I think the reason we keep relitigating this is that the honest version is so short it feels like a trick, and a trick can always be answered with a whitepaper. Next time a vendor promises you both, ask the only question that matters: what does this know about me that I didn't hand over? Sharper means more. Gentler means less. Nobody has ever built the machine that means both, and the reason we keep nodding along is that the only setting on that dial that's genuinely less invasive is the one where the ad doesn't know who you are.
