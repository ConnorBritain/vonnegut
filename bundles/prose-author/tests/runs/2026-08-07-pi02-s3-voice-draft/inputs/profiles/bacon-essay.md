# Voice profile — bacon-essay

## 1. Cadence

The sentence is built by accretion, not by plan. One main clause states the case, then further clauses are hung off it with *for*, *and*, *but*, *so that*, *therefore* — and the sentence ends when the ruling has been delivered, not at any length — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-deformity`: *"Whosoever hath any thing fixed in his person that doth induce contempt, hath also a perpetual spur in himself to rescue and deliver himself from scorn; therefore, all deformed persons are extreme bold"*). To do this: do not decide how long the sentence will be. Keep hanging clauses off the first one until the judgement arrives, then stop on the judgement.

Antithesis is the unit of thought, not an ornament laid over it: a clause says what a thing is, and the next says what its opposite is in the same grammatical frame with one or two words swapped — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"prosperity doth best discover vice, but adversity doth best discover virtue"*; `bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"Young men are fitter to invent than to judge"*). To do this: having written a claim, write it again inverted rather than elaborating it.

Where the members run to three rather than two, they are strictly parallel and the third is the shortest — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Reading maketh a full man; conference a ready man; and writing an exact man"*). The other four build in pairs or in open-ended lists instead.

A sentence beginning *Certainly,* marks the point where the writer stops reporting and delivers his own verdict — 5/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"Certainly it is heaven upon earth, to have a man's mind move in charity"*).

**Typographic, edition-dependent:** those long sentences carry very heavy internal pointing — semicolons, colons and commas at almost every clause joint — 10/10 samples. All ten come from one printed collected edition (`source: Essays, Francis Bacon, 1625 edition (Project Gutenberg, public domain)`), so the marks on the page are a compositor's and a modern transcriber's work. The clause-chaining is the author's and is safe to imitate; the density of the marks is not evidence about him. Repeated in section 8.

## 2. How a piece opens

The subject noun of the essay stands in its first sentence — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"Nature is often hidden, sometimes overcome, seldom extinguished."*). There is no approach to the subject; the first sentence is already on it.

The ten openings fall into three shapes, and every sample is in one of them — I checked all ten:

- A bare general proposition about the subject, asserted as settled — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Studies serve for delight, for ornament, and for ability."*).
- Quoted or reported speech, which the essay then argues with — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"What is truth? said jesting Pilate; and would not stay for an answer."*).
- Clearing the ground by naming what is *not* the subject, or what is wrongly believed about it — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"I mean not to speak of divine prophecies, nor of heathen oracles, nor of natural predictions; but only of prophecies that have been of certain memory"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

No sample ends by restating what it argued — 10/10 samples. The last sentence brings in material that has not appeared before and lets it stand unexplained.

Three of them close on a figure — 3/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"A man's nature runs either to herbs or weeds; therefore, let him seasonably water the one, and destroy the other."*). Three close on a named historical instance, sometimes just a list of names — 3/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"as was M. Regulus, and the two Decii."*). The remaining four simply stop on the last piece of business — a practical instruction, the last of three enumerated causes, a distilled maxim, a scriptural tag.

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The standing subject is a generic *a man* / *men*, and the address is to nobody in particular — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"A man is an ill husband of his honor that entereth into any action…"*).

Advice arrives as a jussive rather than a command to *you* — *let him*, *let a man*, *let there be* — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"let him not set himself too great nor too small tasks"*). Direct second person appears in 3/10 samples, and where it does it is demonstrative rather than hortatory (`bacon-of-building`: *"As you shall see many fine seats set upon a knap of ground"*).

The first person appears in 4/10 samples, and where it appears it is chiefly to fence the claim — to say what will not be argued — rather than to offer an opinion (`bacon-of-faction`: *"but I say not, that the consideration of factions is to be neglected"*; `bacon-of-building`: *"I speak not of many more"*).

## 5. Figures

Images are made of handled and worked things — needlework, husbandry, coin, tackle, food, sport, the trained body — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"natural abilities are like natural plants, that need pruning by study"*; `bacon-of-truth`: *"mixture of falsehood is like alloy in coin of gold and silver"*). The astronomical figures in `bacon-of-faction` and `bacon-of-deformity` are the standing exception to the material, not to the method.

The figure is marked as a figure, with *like* or *as … so* — 9/10 samples (`bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"like an unready horse, that will neither stop nor turn"*). `bacon-of-prophecies` is the one sample with no simile in it.

The figure is discharged inside the sentence that carries it: a connective — *therefore*, *so*, *for* — turns it into the precept it exists to support, and it is not returned to — 7/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"it is more pleasing to have a lively work upon a sad and solemn ground… judge, therefore, of the pleasure of the heart by the pleasure of the eye"*). To do this: write the image, then in the same sentence write *therefore* and the instruction it yields.

## 6. Register range

The register is steady across the corpus, and it moves once. `bacon-of-building` drops the aphoristic mode after its first paragraph for sustained first-person specification with dimensions — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (*"I would have… one only goodly room above stairs, of some forty foot high"*). What shifts it is the subject: a practical undertaking to be carried out, rather than a proposition to be settled.

A second, milder shift is into catalogue — a run of instances with the verdict withheld to the end — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`, `bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`).

Where a piece changes ground it says so, in the same formula both times — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"To pass from theological and philosophical truth to the truth of civil business"*; `bacon-of-building`: *"To pass from the seat to the house itself"*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried inside the sentence: where a piece enumerates, the ordinals sit in running prose — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"First, that men mark when they hit… The second is… The third and last (which is the great one), is…"*). No sample among the ten sets anything off as a heading, a bullet, or a numbered line.

The claim is stated flat and the qualification follows it, as a counter-instance introduced by *but*, *yet* or *though* — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"A man that is young in years may be old in hours, if he have lost no time; but that happeneth rarely."*). Nothing in the corpus softens a claim before making it; there is no hedging apparatus in front of any assertion.

Proof is a named case attached after the claim with *as was* or *as it was with* — 9/10 samples (`bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"as it was with Julius Cæsar and Septimius Severus"*); `bacon-of-studies508` is the one sample carrying no proper name at all. No sample among the ten offers a count, a proportion, or a measured quantity as evidence for a claim — 10/10 samples; the only numbers in the corpus are the dimensions in `bacon-of-building`, which specify a thing rather than support an argument.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is a placeholder with every section deliberately empty, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone with no account from the author to corroborate or contradict — 10/10 samples.

All ten samples carry the same `source` line: one printed collected edition, transcribed. Punctuation, spelling, capitalisation, italics and paragraph division in these texts are therefore a compositor's and a transcriber's, not a manuscript's — 10/10 samples. Nothing here can tell you whether a given semicolon, or the break between paragraph one and paragraph two of `bacon-of-truth`, is the author's. Grammatical observations above survive that; the pointing-density note in section 1 does not, and is repeated here as unreliable.

All ten are the same genre, from the same collection, in one public register. Nothing shows how this voice behaves in a letter, in speech, addressed to a single named person, or when it is not delivering a settled judgement — 10/10 samples.

Every sample uses the archaic inflection — *hath*, *doth*, *maketh*, *seemeth* — 10/10 samples. The corpus cannot separate what is this writer's habit from what is 1625 English, so it cannot tell a drafter whether to reproduce it.

Every sample plants at least one Latin or Italian tag in the running prose — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Abeunt studia in mores"*). What triggers the reach for one, and what governs whether it is translated afterward, is not determinable from ten samples.
