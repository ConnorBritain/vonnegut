# Voice profile — chekhov-correspondence

## 1. Cadence

Sentences get long by accumulation, not by subordination: clauses hooked together with commas and semicolons, each one adding a further concrete particular rather than qualifying the last — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"The river banks are bare, the trees are bare, the earth is a dull brown, there are patches of snow, and there is such a wind that the devil himself could not blow as keenly and hatefully."*; `chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"Rocks, crags, forests, thousands of ducks, herons and all sorts of beaked gentry, and absolute wilderness."*).

What stops a run like that is a short flat sentence, three to eight words, that takes no position and adds no emphasis — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister`, after four lines on the crater: *"I believe in hell now."*; `chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"I stand aside."*). The single sample where the drop never comes is the sustained argument, and that is a register effect (section 6), not a different rhythm.

The other lengthening device is a trailing four-dot ellipsis inside the letter, where a thought is dropped rather than finished — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Rain, cold, mud ... brrr!"*; `chekhov-017-to-a-n-pleshtcheyev`: *"such specialties as convicts, officers, priests...."*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, and no fourth. The first puts the writer's location and physical condition in the first clause — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"The winter is very cold, I am not well, no one has written to me for nearly a whole month"*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"I write to you from Berlin, where I have been now for twenty-four hours."*). The second starts inside the recipient's last letter — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`: *"In your letter there is a text from Scripture."*; `chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail`: *"I got your letter when I was fearfully bored and was sitting at the gate yawning"*). The third opens on a flat general statement that the rest of the paragraph then qualifies — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"I am at home to all commencing, continuing, and concluding authors--that is my rule"*).

Where a salutation survives, it names a role, a nationality-joke or a plural, not the person — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"DEAR ACTRESS,"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The commonest close hands the recipient a small errand, unrelated in scale to what the letter was about — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Ask Lika not to leave such big margins in her letters."*; `chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"Write another twenty stories and send them."*). Relatedly, the last sentence is smaller than the letter around it — a body part, a departure, a gesture — rather than a summary of anything preceding — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister`, after Monte Carlo: *"My heels are sore as it is."*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"Nadyezhda Ivanovna has gone off to Moscow."*).

Two smaller habits, both stated at their true weight. "Well," is the hinge into the sign-off — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"Well, keep well and happy."*). And a signature, where one appears at all, is an invented title rather than a name — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Your bored Volga-travelling / Homo Sachaliensis, / A. CHEKHOV."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The second person is never dropped for long. Every sample turns to the recipient inside the body with a question or an imperative, not only at the edges — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"Don't throw away these sheets"*; `chekhov-017-to-a-n-pleshtcheyev`: *"Put yourself in the place of a corncrake"*). The direct question is the main instrument, and it often doubles — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"And you, why are you depressed? What are you depressed about?"*).

Distance is set by the vocative, and the vocatives are roles or jokes dropped mid-sentence — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"and no wonder, your Excellency!"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"Have you taken a house for the holidays, signori?"*).

The punctuation that does the most work is the dash, which carries a turn or a gloss where a colon would otherwise sit — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"I was ill and undressed--forgive these homely details--I was in my bedroom"*). Its most fixed form is a dash plus *that is* — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"to indulge my talent--that is, to sleep."*).

## 5. Figures

Comparisons come from food, animals, servants, relatives and the body — the material of the household and the road — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"she eats mechanically, as though she were munching oats"*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"I am in the condition of a transplanted tree which is hesitating whether to take root or to begin to wither"*). A figure runs one clause and is then abandoned; the next sentence is about something else — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"its whistle is midway between the bray of an ass and an Aeolian harp. In five or six hours we shall be in Nizhni."*).

The exception is a long comic personification given a whole family of dependents — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"steam-tugs ... look like some fine young intellectual trying to run away while a plebeian wife, mother-in-law, sister-in-law, and wife's grandmother hold on to his coat-tails."*). Often the figure is simply a price, stated exactly and left to do the describing — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"for fourteen or sixteen marks a day we have a double room flooded with sunshine"*).

## 6. Register range

The corpus moves with the recipient, and what moves is the ratio of joke to proposition. Letters home are inventories of food, cold, money and bodies with a joke every few sentences — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"Oh, how badly the German women dress!"*). Letters answering another writer on craft drop the jokes entirely and run as propositions — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-087-to-madame-avilov`: *"The more objective, the stronger will be the effect."*).

Constant across both: self-deprecation delivered as fact — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"I am at a complete loss before them, and recognise my bankruptcy."*). So is complaint that undercuts itself inside the same sentence — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"I am doing nothing because I intend to set to work."*).

One sample sustains an argument by anaphora and never turns aside — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`: *"I don't believe in our educated class ... I don't believe in it even when it's suffering and complaining ... I believe in individual people"*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried by the dateline: when the writer moves, the letter restarts under a new place and date and simply continues — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"BADENWEILER, / June 12."*). Nothing here breaks a piece up with a heading, a numbered list or a bullet.

A change of subject is marked by starting a new paragraph with the new subject's name in the first two words — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Kostroma is a nice town."*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"I live on the ground floor."*). No sample announces a transition with a phrase describing what will follow.

Judgements arrive as a bare predicate in first position, with the evidence supplied afterwards — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"The Kama is a very dull river. To realise its beauties one would have to be a native..."*). No verdict in the corpus is built up to, and none is placed after the reasoning that supports it.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is an unfilled placeholder, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone with no statement of intent to check it against — 10/10 samples.

Every sample is Constance Garnett's English and carries `translated: true`; the dash, the four-dot ellipsis and idioms like *"Well, I can't help it...."* may be hers rather than the author's, and this profile cannot separate the two layers — 10/10 samples.

Four samples begin with an editorial *"..."* marking a cut, so their real first sentences are absent and section 2 rests on the six that are whole — 4/10 samples.

Every recipient is a family member, a fellow writer, an editor, or a woman he is close to; nothing here shows the voice addressing a stranger, an institution, or a hostile reader — 10/10 samples.

Every sample is a letter with one named recipient, so nothing in sections 2, 3 or 4 can be assumed to transfer to a form with no addressee — 10/10 samples.

Four samples are compilations of several dated letters under one file; counts above treat each file as one sample, so a habit occurring six times inside one file still counts once — 4/10 samples.

The samples span 1876 to 1904 with no two from the same year, so nothing here distinguishes a lifelong habit from one held only briefly — 10/10 samples.
