# Voice profile — chekhov-correspondence

Ten usable samples, all carrying `source`, `date`, `human_authored: true`. Nothing was excluded. `voice.md` is an unfilled placeholder, so everything below is derived from the corpus.

## 1. Cadence

The characteristic movement is a long clause-run — commas and semicolons piling clauses without subordination — landing on a short flat sentence that stops the run dead: 9/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"I looked at five sheets, handled them, and did not take one. I am going to-day to buy rubber overshoes."*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"You go on and on and on, and it is still a long way to the top. ... I believe in hell now."*). The exception is `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`, which sustains one argument to the end and never lets a short sentence land — the pattern breaks when he is arguing rather than reporting.

Inside those runs, the standard unit is a catalogue of three or more concrete items closed by a dismissive sweep — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"Ashes, mountains of lava, solid waves of molten minerals, mounds of earth, and every sort of abomination."*; `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`: *"doctors owning house property, hungry government clerks, and thieving engineers"*). The tail is not decoration; it is where the judgment sits.

A double-dash gloss, usually the words *that is*, restates a phrase in plainer terms mid-sentence — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"to indulge my talent--that is, to sleep"*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"a cup of strawberry tea--that is as a sleeping draught"*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, and no composite of them.

Taking up something the recipient wrote or did, before any news of his own — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail`: *"I got your letter when I was fearfully bored and was sitting at the gate yawning"*; `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`: *"In your letter there is a text from Scripture."*).

A flat statement of where he is and what the weather is doing — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"The Amur is a very fine river"*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"The winter is very cold, I am not well"*).

A verdict on another writer, or a rule of his own stated as fact — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"I am at home to all commencing, continuing, and concluding authors--that is my rule"*).

Four of the ten begin mid-thought, an ellipsis standing where the salutation would be — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-087-to-madame-avilov`: *"... Yes, it is nice now in the country"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The last sentence is a small unglamorous particular, usually smaller than what preceded it — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister`, after Monte Carlo: *"My heels are sore as it is."*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"Nadyezhda Ivanovna has gone off to Moscow."*).

The closing gesture is an imperative pointed at the recipient — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Ask Lika not to leave such big margins in her letters."*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"Write!"*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

Direct questions are fired at the recipient and not answered for them — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper`: *"And you, why are you depressed? What are you depressed about?"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"How have you settled the summer villa question? Is the mongoose alive?"*).

The four-dot ellipsis is the workhorse punctuation, marking a thought dropped rather than finished — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"but you were offended. Well, I can't help it...."*; `chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"How is one to describe them? ..."*). `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper` is the only sample without one.

Intimates are addressed by a collective joke-name rather than by name — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"Have you taken a house for the holidays, signori?"*).

## 5. Figures

Comparisons are announced with *like* or *as though* and left in one sentence — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"she eats mechanically, as though she were munching oats"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"here in Rome we live like Cardinals"*).

What the images are made of is animals, food, and the body — never abstractions — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail`: *"an unpleasant sensation which mortals feel after eating too many raisins or currants"*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"Health is coming back to me not by ounces but by stones."*).

## 6. Register range

The voice moves, and the recipient is what moves it. To household intimates the letter becomes an inventory of weather, food, money, and the body, in that repeating rotation — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"I am still eating butter in enormous quantities and with no effect. I can't take milk."*).

Money is named in exact figures inside those inventories — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister`: *"costing us forty francs a day"*).

Where the subject is an argument or a schedule, a repeated sentence-opening frame stacks three or more times — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov`: *"I don't believe in our educated class... I don't believe in it even when it's suffering... I believe in individual people"*).

Exclamation marks carry the mood and vanish entirely when he instructs or argues — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"Oh, the caviare!"*; `chekhov-017-to-a-n-pleshtcheyev`: *"Ah, if only we had decent critics!"*). The four without any are the four that give advice or make a case.

## 7. What the corpus never does

A change of day or city is marked by a new dateline and a blank gap, with no transition sentence carrying the reader across — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154-to-his-sister` moves from Berlin to *"BADENWEILER, June 12."* and continues as if mid-conversation). Nothing in the corpus breaks a letter up with a heading, a numbered point, or a bulleted list.

A judgment and its reversal share one sentence, hinged on *but* or *though* — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin`: *"They are dressed poorly, but beautifully"*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister`: *"The whole dinner is magnificent, but the maitres d'hotel look so important that it makes one uneasy."*). No sample states a judgment and then walks it back in a following sentence.

People, towns and books are named flatly and left unexplained, the reader assumed to know or not to need to — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister`: *"All the cabmen are like Dobrolyubov."*). Only `chekhov-073-to-e-m-s` names nobody.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is an unfilled placeholder in every section, so there is no authorial account to corroborate or contradict; all of the above is corpus-derived — 10/10 samples.

The text is Constance Garnett's translation, per `profile.json`. Punctuation, idiom and clause joinery are partly hers, so every cadence and punctuation observation above inherits that caveat — 10/10 samples.

Every sample is a letter to one named person. Nothing here shows how the voice behaves addressing a public, a stranger, or an institution — 10/10 samples.

Cool distance rests on a single sample — 1/10 samples, the only place it appears (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s`: *"And so--to see you was physically impossible... but you were offended. Well, I can't help it...."*). How the voice sustains coolness beyond one short letter is not determinable here.

Change over time cannot be separated from change of recipient: only one sample predates 1888 — 1/10 samples (`chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail`, 1876) — and it is also the only letter to a child.

Seven candidate observations were dropped for lack of two citable samples, including a signature habit and a pattern of self-deprecation about his own writing, each holding in only two samples and not stated plainly enough to survive.
