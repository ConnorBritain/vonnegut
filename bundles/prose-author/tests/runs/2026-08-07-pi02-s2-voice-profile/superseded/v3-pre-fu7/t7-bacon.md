# Voice profile — bacon-essay

## 1. Cadence

The unit is not the sentence but the clause, and clauses are strung on semicolons until the thought runs out. Every sample builds at least one sentence of four or more clauses hinged this way — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Reading maketh a full man; conference a ready man; and writing an exact man"*). The semicolon does the work a paragraph break would do elsewhere: it holds parallel items in one breath instead of separating them.

The engine inside those chains is antithesis — a pair set against itself, usually with the verb repeated across the pivot — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"prosperity doth best discover vice, but adversity doth best discover virtue"*). When the pair widens it becomes a run of three or more parallel members, often anaphoric — 8/10 samples (`bacon-of-building`: *"want of water, want of wood, shade, and shelter, want of fruitfulness"*).

Against that length, a bare sentence under ten words lands where a general rule is being set down and nothing is being argued yet — 4/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"Nature is often hidden, sometimes overcome, seldom extinguished."*). So what makes the cadence change is the job: stating a rule shortens the line, working through cases lengthens it.

Half the samples never break at all — one paragraph from first word to last, some of them a thousand words long — 5/10 samples (`bacon-of-deformity`, a single block). Where a break does occur it is announced by the sentence itself before the white space arrives — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"To pass from theological and philosophical truth to the truth of civil business"*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, no composite.

Most often the first sentence is a flat general proposition, delivered as settled fact with no approach to it — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Studies serve for delight, for ornament, and for ability."*).

Second, the piece opens inside someone else's speech and works outward from it — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"What is truth? said jesting Pilate; and would not stay for an answer."*).

Third, it opens by refusing a position — naming what will not be discussed, or the wrong opinion, before anything is asserted — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"I mean not to speak of divine prophecies, nor of heathen oracles, nor of natural predictions"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The ending is a landing, not a summary. Most often it lands on a compressed figure that has not appeared before — 4/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"A man's nature runs either to herbs or weeds; therefore, let him seasonably water the one, and destroy the other."*).

Equally often it lands on borrowed authority: a Latin or scriptural tag, or a bare list of names offered as proof — 4/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"as was M. Regulus, and the two Decii."*).

The remainder simply stop on the last piece of practical instruction, unornamented — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-building`: *"let them stand at distance, with some low galleries to pass from them to the palace itself."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The standing subject is a generic third person, *a man*, and instruction is aimed at him rather than at the reader — 7/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"A man is an ill husband of his honor that entereth into any action…"*). Advice arrives in the third-person jussive rather than the imperative — 5/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"let him study the mathematics"*).

*You* is rare and appears where the reader is being shown something physical or asked to test a claim — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-building`: *"As you shall see many fine seats set upon a knap of ground…"*). *I* is rarer still, and in every instance marks the limit of what will be said or claimed, never a personal experience offered for its own sake — 4/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"There was also another prophecy before the year of eighty-eight, which I do not well understand."*).

The distance is held by parentheses, which carry the qualifications, the sources, and the second thoughts, so the main clause never has to slow for them — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-faction`: *"like the motions (as the astronomers speak) of the inferior orbs"*).

## 5. Figures

An image is introduced by an explicit *like* or *as*, and it arrives after the abstract claim, to fix it rather than replace it — 8/10 samples (`bacon-of-truth`: *"mixture of falsehood is like alloy in coin of gold and silver, which may make the metal work the better, but it embaseth it"*).

The material is trades and household things — needlework, coining, pruning, bowling, horsemanship, archery, swimming — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"let him practise with helps, as swimmers do with bladders, or rushes; but, after a time, let him practise with disadvantages, as dancers do with thick shoes"*). Where a figure is not a simile it is a named person doing something: exempla stand in for generalization — 9/10 samples (`bacon-of-deformity`: *"as was Agesilaüs, Zanger, the son of Solyman, Æsop, Gasca president of Peru"*). Latin runs inline as a quoted tag, sometimes glossed and often not — 9/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Abeunt studia in mores"*).

## 6. Register range

The corpus moves, and it moves by subject. One sample abandons proposition for physical specification, with measurements and materials — 1/10 samples — the only place this appears (`bacon-of-building`: *"one only goodly room above stairs, of some forty foot high"*). One other abandons argument for a catalogue of instances laid end to end, holding the verdict until the last third — 1/10 samples — the only place this appears (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"Domitian dreamed, the night before he was slain, that a golden head was growing out of the nape of his neck"*).

What survives both shifts is the impersonal third person: no sample speaks from a first-person situation or names a reader — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"A man that is young in years may be old in hours, if he have lost no time"*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Sequence is carried by ordinals inside the running sentence, so the structure is audible without being visible — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"First, that men mark when they hit, and never mark when they miss"*). Nothing here breaks a piece with a heading, a bullet, or a numbered line; even the long specification runs as continuous paragraphs.

Transitions are hinged with a single short conjunction or a bare *therefore* at the head of the clause — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-deformity`: *"Again, in their superiors, it quencheth jealousy towards them"*). No sample reaches for a Latinate discourse adverb of the *moreover / furthermore / in conclusion* family to do that work.

The last sentence adds — a figure, a name, a citation not used before — so the piece ends heavier than it was — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"virtue is like precious odors, most fragrant when they are incensed, or crushed"*). None of the ten closes by restating what it argued.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

The voice card is unfilled, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone — 10/10 samples.

All ten are the same genre, the same medium, and the same year, so nothing here shows the voice in a letter, in dialogue, or writing to a deadline — 10/10 samples.

No sample addresses a named person; the *you* of `bacon-of-building` is a generic builder. Nothing shows how the voice behaves toward a known reader, an opponent, or a subordinate — 10/10 samples.

The punctuation habits reported in sections 1 and 4 may not be the author's. Every sample carries the same modernizing edition in its provenance line while printing *honor* and *favor*, and semicolon density is exactly the thing such editions change — 10/10 samples.
