# Voice profile — chekhov-correspondence

## 1. How the sentences run

The default unit is a long sentence assembled by accumulation — clause on clause, joined with commas, semicolons and *and* — which then stops dead and is followed by a very short flat statement of fact. The long clause does the seeing; the short one does the reporting, and the drop between them is where the voice lives — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-154`: *"my asthma is better, nothing is aching."*; `chekhov-031`: *"The sun is rising. I slept last night artistically. My money is safe."*).

Inside the long sentences, the commonest device is a catalogue of bare nouns, frequently with no verb attached at all — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-043`: *"Rocks, crags, forests, thousands of ducks, herons and all sorts of beaked gentry, and absolute wilderness."*; `chekhov-031`: *"Rain, cold, mud ... brrr!"*).

A sentence very often ends on a four-dot trailing ellipsis and the paragraph then continues on something else, so a thought is closed by fading rather than by a full stop — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-073`: *"but you were offended. Well, I can't help it...."*). See section 8 for why this one carries a caveat.

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, not one. First, a dateline followed by a shouted vocative — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-073`: *"HONOURED ELENA MIHAILOVNA,"*).

Second, and more usefully for a drafter: the first sentence of the body puts the writer somewhere physical, or reports the weather, before it does anything else — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-132`: *"The winter is very cold, I am not well, no one has written to me for nearly a whole month"*; `chekhov-154`: *"I write to you from Berlin, where I have been now for twenty-four hours."*).

Third, in the only place this appears, the letter opens by asking the recipient a question about their weather rather than reporting his own — 1/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"Did you have rain when Ivan was coming back from the monastery?"*).

## 3. How a piece closes

There is no summation. The last sentence is the same size as the ones before it — a small material fact, an aside, a price, a body part — and the letter simply stops on it — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-132`: *"Nadyezhda Ivanovna has gone off to Moscow."*; `chekhov-056`: *"My heels are sore as it is."*).

When the ending is aimed rather than dropped, it is an imperative pointed at the recipient — keep well, write, don't forget me — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154`: *"Well, keep well and happy. My love to Mother, Vanya, George, and all the rest. Write!"*).

Two of the ten retain a signature block; one of those two invents a role-name for the writer instead of using his own — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"Your bored Volga-travelling / Homo Sachaliensis, / A. CHEKHOV."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

One named person, present in the room. The clearest marker is the direct question, which expects an answer and is not rhetorical — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-056`: *"How have you settled the summer villa question? Is the mongoose alive?"*; `chekhov-001`: *"Why, you are not a rascal, you are an honest man, aren't you?"*).

The recipient is also named or titled *inside* a sentence, mid-letter, often mock-formally — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-043`: *"Everything in my head is muddled; and no wonder, your Excellency!"*; `chekhov-056`: *"You, Masha, and you, Lika, would be rabid with delight."*).

Instructions are given bare, in the imperative, without softening — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-001`: *"Read "Don Quixote." It is a fine thing."*; `chekhov-031`: *"Ask Lika not to leave such big margins in her letters."*).

The punctuation that carries the turn is the double dash, most often introducing a self-correction or a plain-language gloss of the phrase just used — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"I made it my first duty to indulge my talent--that is, to sleep."*; `chekhov-115`: *"Not the tutor but the whole educated class--that is to blame, my dear sir."*).

## 5. Figures

Comparisons are built out of domestic, bodily, animal and household material — food, livestock, servants, relatives, coffee, money — and the more elevated the thing being described, the lower the material used to describe it — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"Very beautiful are the steam-tugs, dragging after them four or five barges each; they look like some fine young intellectual trying to run away while a plebeian wife, mother-in-law, sister-in-law, and wife's grandmother hold on to his coat-tails."*; `chekhov-132`: *"I am in the condition of a transplanted tree which is hesitating whether to take root or to begin to wither."*).

Exaggeration, when it comes, is given an exact number rather than an intensifier — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-056`: *"you feel that you are not at a hairdresser's but at the Vatican where there are eleven thousand rooms"*; `chekhov-031`: *"I have been two and a half years sailing to Perm, so it seems to me."*).

## 6. Register range

What moves the register is the recipient. Writing to family, the letter is an inventory: food, money, cold, the state of his legs, comic abuse of strangers — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154`: *"I am still eating butter in enormous quantities and with no effect."*).

Writing to editors and other writers, the same voice states a general proposition and then applies it, and the sentences lengthen to carry the argument — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-087`: *"The more objective, the stronger will be the effect."*; `chekhov-115`: *"I believe in individual people, I see salvation in individual personalities scattered here and there all over Russia"*).

Within a single letter, the movement is complaint undercut immediately by a joke at the writer's own expense, usually in the very next sentence — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-056`: *"I bought you a watch, Masha, but I have cast it to the swine. But there, God forgive you!"*; `chekhov-031`: *"Well, I thought, this one will certainly murder me. It appeared that this was our relation A. M. S."*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Feeling is reported as a physical fact and then left alone — the state is named in one clause, given a bodily detail, and the paragraph moves to something else — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"I am neither gay nor bored, but there is a sort of numbness in my soul. I like to sit without moving or speaking."*). Nothing in the corpus then explains what the feeling meant or what the recipient should make of it.

Figures are placed at the end of their paragraph and abandoned there; the next paragraph changes subject — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"She eats no less than other people, but she eats mechanically, as though she were munching oats."* — followed by *"Kostroma is a nice town."*). No comparison in the corpus is reopened, unpacked, or followed by *which is to say* or an equivalent.

Joins between subjects are done with white space and a new dateline — the letters are serial, written in sittings, and a change of day does the work a transition would otherwise do — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-154`: a bare *"June 16."* between the Badenweiler regime and the German band). Nothing in the corpus opens a paragraph by announcing what that paragraph is going to do.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

The four-dot ellipsis recorded in section 1 is the least safe observation here. Four of the ten samples also *begin* with a leading `...`, which is plainly an editor's elision — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-115`, `chekhov-087`, `chekhov-017`, `chekhov-154`). Mid-paragraph ellipses may therefore be the editor cutting rather than the author trailing off, and this corpus cannot separate the two.

The corpus is a translation. Sentence joinery, dash use, exclamation marks and idiom are partly Constance Garnett's; every punctuation-level observation above inherits that.

Openings could not be observed at all in the four elided samples, so section 2 rests on six.

`voice.md` is unfilled — every observation above is derived from the corpus alone, and there is no statement of intent to corroborate or contradict it.

Not determinable from this corpus: how the voice behaves at length in a single sitting (the long samples are serial, not continuous); how it handles a hostile or unknown recipient (`chekhov-073` is the only strained one); how it opens or closes anything that is not a letter.

Observations considered and dropped for want of citation: 7.
