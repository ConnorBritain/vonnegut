# Voice profile — bacon-essay

## 1. Cadence

The sentence is the unit of composition and it is long. Clauses are strung with semicolons and colons and kept running by `for`, `but`, `yet`, `so as`, `therefore`; the sentence does not end where the thought could have ended, it ends when the qualifications are exhausted — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-faction`: *"those that are seconds in factions do many times, when the faction subdivideth, prove principals; but many times also they prove ciphers, and cashiered, for many a man's strength is in opposition; and when that faileth, he groweth out of use."*).

What makes the length bearable is that the chain is almost always built out of matched halves. Two things are set against each other in the same grammar, so the reader hears the second coming — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"prosperity doth best discover vice, but adversity doth best discover virtue"*; `bacon-of-truth`: *"For a lie faces God, and shrinks from man"*).

Where the pairing widens it widens to three, and the third member is where the sentence lands — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Reading maketh a full man; conference a ready man; and writing an exact man"*; `bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"Nature is often hidden, sometimes overcome, seldom extinguished."*). Three is the ceiling in these six; beyond three the list stops being rhythmic and becomes an inventory (section 6).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, not one.

Most often the first sentence is the thesis stated flat, with no approach to it — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Studies serve for delight, for ornament, and for ability."*; `bacon-of-deformity`: *"Deformed persons are commonly even with nature"*).

Second, the essay opens on someone else's words and takes them as the occasion — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"It was a high speech of Seneca (after the manner of the Stoics), that..."*; `bacon-of-truth`: *"What is truth? said jesting Pilate; and would not stay for an answer."*).

Third, the first sentence fences the subject by naming what is being excluded, or by naming an opinion to be corrected — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"I mean not to speak of divine prophecies, nor of heathen oracles, nor of natural predictions; but only of..."*; `bacon-of-faction`: *"Many have an opinion not wise, that..."*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The last sentence brings in something that has not been in the essay before — a new image, a new example, a new quotation — and the piece stops there. Nothing is gathered up, and no closing sentence in the corpus refers back to the essay as a whole — 10/10 samples (`bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"A man's nature runs either to herbs or weeds; therefore, let him seasonably water the one, and destroy the other."*).

One recurring version of this: the essay ends on a roll of proper names, with the argument left where it stood — 3/10 samples (`bacon-of-deformity`: *"as was Agesilaüs, Zanger, the son of Solyman, Æsop, Gasca president of Peru; and Socrates may go likewise amongst them, with others."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The subject of the sentence is usually a generic third person, `a man`, and the advice is fitted to him rather than to a reader — 7/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"A man is an ill husband of his honor that entereth into any action..."*).

Instruction, when it comes, is given as an imperative or a `let` hortative rather than as a recommendation — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"Read not to contradict and confute, nor to believe and take for granted, nor to find talk and discourse, but to weigh and consider."*; `bacon-of-building`: *"Let the court not be paved"*).

The first person appears, but only to manage the essay — to declare scope, to decline a topic, to register a judgment on the material — 4/10 samples (`bacon-of-building`: *"I speak not of many more"*; `bacon-of-prophecies`: *"My judgment is, that they ought all to be despised"*). In the only place any autobiographical detail appears, it is offered as the provenance of an example and nothing more — 1/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"When I was in France, I heard from one Dr. Pena, that the queen mother, who was given to curious arts..."*).

## 5. Figures

An image arrives as a simile, marked with `like` or with the `as ... so` frame, and it is a short one — the comparison is made and the sentence moves on — 8/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"Honor that is gained and broken upon another hath the quickest reflection, like diamonds cut with facets"*; `bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"like an unready horse, that will neither stop nor turn"*).

The material of the images is domestic and handled: needlework, coin, shoes, chimneys, garden weeds, distilled water — 7/10 samples (`bacon-of-adversity`: *"We see, in needleworks and embroideries, it is more pleasing to have a lively work upon a sad and solemn ground"*; `bacon-of-nature-in-men`: *"let him practise with helps, as swimmers do with bladders, or rushes"*).

Alongside this, a Latin tag is dropped into the sentence and left to stand in Latin, sometimes with an English version beside it and often without — 9/10 samples (`bacon-of-studies508`: *"logic and rhetoric, able to contend: 'Abeunt studia in mores;'"*; `bacon-of-deformity`: *"'Ubi peccat in uno, periclitatur in altero.'"*).

## 6. Register range

The corpus shows two departures from the aphoristic mode, and both are driven by the subject rather than by mood.

When the subject is enumerable, the prose becomes a ranked catalogue: the argument is suspended, the instances are numbered in order, and the judgment is held to the end — 2/10 samples (`bacon-of-honor-and-reputation`: *"In the first place are 'conditores imperiorum'... in the second place are 'legislatores'"*).

In the only place the subject is a physical object, the prose turns into sustained specification — measurements, materials, dispositions of rooms — while the semicolon chain and the `let` imperative continue unchanged — 1/10 samples (`bacon-of-building`: *"I would have it two stories, of eighteen foot high apiece above the two wings; and a goodly leads upon the top, railed, with statues interposed"*). Mood, recipient, and occasion do not visibly shift anywhere in this corpus; see section 8.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Enumeration is carried inside the sentence, with the ordinals doing the work that layout would otherwise do, and six of the ten essays are a single unbroken block of prose — 6/10 samples (`bacon-of-prophecies`: *"consisteth in three things. First, that men mark when they hit... The second is... The third and last (which is the great one), is..."*). Nothing in the corpus uses a heading, a bullet, or a numbered display list; the ordinals are the structure.

Generalizations about classes of people are asserted as plain predicates in the main clause — 8/10 samples (`bacon-of-youth-and-age`: *"Men of age object too much, consult too long, adventure too little, repent too soon"*). Where a claim is softened, the softening is a single word dropped inside the sentence — `commonly`, `perhaps`, `for the most part`, `lightly` — and it never occupies the front of the sentence or displaces the assertion from the main clause; no hedging frame precedes a claim anywhere in the corpus.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is a set of empty placeholders, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone; there is no statement of intent to corroborate or contradict it.

All ten samples share one medium, one edition, and one date — 10/10 samples carry `date: 1625-01-01`. So this profile has no evidence at all about how the prose moves when the recipient changes, when the occasion is private, when the writing is unrevised, or when the piece is short. Section 6 reports what shifts with subject only; a claim that this voice is fixed would be a claim about the corpus, not about the writing.

Punctuation observations are unavoidably partly the modern editor's: the source is a Project Gutenberg text with normalized spelling and pointing, and this profile cannot separate authorial semicolons from editorial ones. The clause-chaining in section 1 is safe because it is grammatical, not typographic; the specific density of semicolons is not.

Eight candidate observations were dropped for want of support or because a spot check falsified them.
