# Voice profile — chekhov-correspondence

*Ten usable samples, all carrying provenance. Confidence: full. The author's voice card is empty, so everything below is derived from the corpus alone.*

## 1. How the sentences run

The default sentence is long and made by accretion: short independent clauses set side by side with commas and semicolons, rarely subordinated to each other — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"he is editor of the_ Ekaterinburg Week_ which is under the censorship of the police-master Baron Taube, is married and has two children, is growing rich and getting fat and elderly, and lives in a 'substantial way.'"*; `chekhov-073`: *"Even if it were not so, even if for some reason I did not desire your visit, even then I should have received you."*). The clauses are of similar weight, so the sentence reads as a list being added to rather than an argument being built.

That run is then stopped by a very short flat sentence, usually under eight words, which takes the paragraph's air out — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"I stand aside."*; `chekhov-056`: *"I believe in hell now."*; `chekhov-132`: *"I dig in the garden."*). The short sentence is not a summary of the long one. It is a different, smaller fact placed next to it.

What moves the length is the subject. Advising, arguing, or describing landscape lengthens the clauses and multiplies them; reporting the body, the weather, money, or the timetable shortens them to bare declaratives — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-154`, where the argument about German taste runs on and the health report goes *"I can't take milk."*; `chekhov-115`, an argument with almost no short sentence in it).

## 2. How a piece opens

Three shapes, and they do not blend.

Four open on a salutation that names the recipient with a joke or an honorific attached — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-132`: *"DEAR ACTRESS,"*; `chekhov-073`: *"HONOURED ELENA MIHAILOVNA,"*).

Four begin mid-conversation, answering something the recipient wrote, with no re-establishment of what is being answered — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-115`: *"... In your letter there is a text from Scripture."*; `chekhov-087`: *"... Yes, it is nice now in the country, not only nice but positively amazing."*).

Two open on a bare declarative about the immediate situation, verb early, no throat-clearing — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-043`: *"The Amur is a very fine river"*; `chekhov-056`: *"When I got to Rome I went to the post-office and did not find a single letter."*).

## 3. How a piece closes

The commonest close deflates: the last sentence is smaller than what came before it — a body complaint, an errand, a piece of household news — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031`, which ends a Siberian journey on *"Ask Lika not to leave such big margins in her letters."*; `chekhov-056`, which ends Monte Carlo on *"My heels are sore as it is."*; `chekhov-132`: *"Nadyezhda Ivanovna has gone off to Moscow."*).

The alternative close lands a flat general statement, unattributed and unqualified — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-017`: *"Life is hard in this world!"*; `chekhov-087`: *"The more objective, the stronger will be the effect."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

Second person throughout, and the recipient is asked questions that expect an answer rather than questions for effect — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-056`: *"How have you settled the summer villa question? Is the mongoose alive?"*; `chekhov-132`: *"And you, why are you depressed? What are you depressed about?"*; `chekhov-073`: *"But can you really have written only fifteen stories?"*).

The recipient gets named inside the sentence, mid-flow, as a nudge or a jab — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-056`: *"You, Masha, and you, Lika, would be rabid with delight."*; `chekhov-115`: *"that is to blame, my dear sir"*; `chekhov-001`: *"You won't understand it, my dear."*).

Groups of family are addressed by an invented collective title, mock-grand — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-056`: *"All the summer, noble gentlemen"* and *"Have you taken a house for the holidays, signori?"*).

The double dash carries a self-interruption that corrects or narrows what was just said — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"indulge my talent--that is, to sleep"*; `chekhov-154`: *"by anything but fasting--that is, eating nothing--and that's the end of it"*; `chekhov-056`: *"thought of_ her_--that is, of Lika L."*).

## 5. Figures

Comparisons are built out of domestic, animal and bodily material — food, livestock, servants, ailments — not out of literature or abstraction — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"she eats mechanically, as though she were munching oats"*; *"its whistle is midway between the bray of an ass and an Aeolian harp"*; `chekhov-001`: *"an unpleasant sensation which mortals feel after eating too many raisins or currants"*; `chekhov-154`: *"Health is coming back to me not by ounces but by stones."*).

When a figure is extended it becomes a small scene with a cast, not a longer adjective — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"they look like some fine young intellectual trying to run away while a plebeian wife, mother-in-law, sister-in-law, and wife's grandmother hold on to his coat-tails"*; `chekhov-132`: *"the condition of a transplanted tree which is hesitating whether to take root or to begin to wither"*).

## 6. Register range

Inside a single paragraph, enthusiasm is followed immediately by its own puncture — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-043`: *"There is beauty, space, freedom and warmth"* then *"but I am not equal to it"*; `chekhov-154`: *"The whole dinner is magnificent, but the maitres d'hotel look so important that it makes one uneasy."*).

When the subject turns to how writing should be done, the sentence contracts into a short balanced impersonal rule — 3/10 samples (`chekhov-087`: *"The more objective, the stronger will be the effect."*; `chekhov-017`: *"This is not specialization but partiality."*).

Letters to family are built as inventory — places, prices, meals, people met, in the order they occurred — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-154`, which runs the whole day's regime hour by hour; `chekhov-031`, the same for a river journey). The letters to writers keep the same syntax and the same jokes; what changes is the subject, not the voice.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Images are left to stand. A comparison is made and the next sentence moves to something else — 7/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"White gulls, looking like the younger Drishka, hover over the water. The steamer is not up to much...."*). The one gloss construction present, *--that is,* glosses facts, never images.

Feeling is reported as physical fact and then dropped — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"there is a sort of numbness in my soul. I like to sit without moving or speaking."*; `chekhov-056`: *"the thought of that spoils my appetite"*). No passage stays with a mood to examine it.

Verdicts arrive bare, with no frame in front of them — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-031`: *"The Volga is not bad"*, *"They are not gifted persons."*; `chekhov-154`: *"turns out to be skilful and nice"*). Nothing in the corpus prefaces a judgement with a hedge of the *it seems to me that* kind; the judgement is the whole sentence.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is a placeholder, so nothing here is corroborated against the author's own account of the voice — and 10/10 samples are marked `translated: true` and come from one translator, so contractions, dash usage and idiom may be hers rather than his.

The samples are excerpts: a four-dot ellipsis marks removed text in 7/10 samples (`chekhov-115` opens on one). Whole-letter architecture and paragraph-to-paragraph transition therefore cannot be read reliably from this corpus; the opening and closing observations above are of surviving openings and closings.

Formality under strain appears in the only place it can be seen — 1/10 samples (`chekhov-073`, to someone who has taken offence: *"forgive these homely details"*, and a curt *"Write another twenty stories and send them."*). One sample cannot say whether that is a habit.

Change over the career is undetermined: 1/10 samples predates 1888 (`chekhov-001`, 1876), so the corpus is effectively one sixteen-year window plus an outlier.
