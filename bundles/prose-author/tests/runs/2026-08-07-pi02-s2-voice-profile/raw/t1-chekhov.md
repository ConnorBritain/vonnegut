# Voice profile — chekhov-correspondence

This corpus is a translated, selected edition (Constance Garnett; Project Gutenberg). Everything below that concerns punctuation, ellipses, dashes or paragraph breaks may belong to the translator or the selecting editor rather than the author, and is flagged where it appears. Grammar and structure are reported normally.

## 1. How the sentences run

The engine of this prose is accretion: a sentence takes a subject and then adds items in series until the series has done the work, three or more at a time — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin.txt`: *"Rocks, crags, forests, thousands of ducks, herons and all sorts of beaked gentry, and absolute wilderness."*; `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov.txt`: *"all that is left in the filter is doctors owning house property, hungry government clerks, and thieving engineers"*). The items are always things, never qualities. To do this: when you would reach for one summarising adjective, list four particulars instead and stop without a summary.

What ends an accumulating sentence is a short flat one that takes the air out of it — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`, after two sentences of water meadows and sunlit monasteries: *"The steamer is not up to much."*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`, after the whole ascent of Vesuvius: *"I believe in hell now."*). The one sample that does not deflate is `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov.txt`, which is sustained argument throughout. Rule of thumb for a drafter: if a sentence has run long and enthusiastic, make the next one under eight words and unimpressed.

Statements are set beside each other rather than ranked — two independent observations share a sentence with no conjunction telling you which one governs — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin.txt`: *"The Amur is a very fine river; I have gained more from it than I could have expected"*). *Caveat: the semicolons and dashes carrying this are the translator's; the parataxis is structural and survives her.*

A term gets restated more narrowly a beat after it is used — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: *"I made it my first duty to indulge my talent--that is, to sleep."*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister.txt`: *"cure it by anything but fasting--that is, eating nothing"*). The dash is the edition's; the move — say it grandly, then say what it actually was — is not.

## 2. How a letter opens

The first sentence is already inside its subject; no sample spends a sentence arriving — 10/10 samples. Across the ten, the openings are of three kinds, and I checked every sample against the split.

Four open on the correspondence itself, present or missing — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`: *"When I got to Rome I went to the post-office and did not find a single letter."*; `chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail.txt`: *"I got your letter when I was fearfully bored"*).

Four open on a flat verdict about a place, the weather, or his own condition — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper.txt`: *"The winter is very cold, I am not well, no one has written to me for nearly a whole month"*).

Two open by stating a rule of his own before anything else — 2/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s.txt`: *"I am at home to all commencing, continuing, and concluding authors--that is my rule"*).

## 3. How a letter closes

Six close smaller than they were running: the last sentence is a practical instruction or a small physical fact, deliberately beneath the paragraph before it — 6/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`: *"I am weary of wandering over the face of the earth. One must draw the line. My heels are sore as it is."*; `chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: *"Ask Lika not to leave such big margins in her letters."*).

The other four close upward, on a general statement — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-017-to-a-n-pleshtcheyev.txt`: *"Life is hard in this world!"*; `chekhov-087-to-madame-avilov.txt`: *"The more objective, the stronger will be the effect."*).

## 4. Who is being addressed

One named person, in the second person, kept awake by being asked things: a direct question to the recipient appears in 7/10 samples (`chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper.txt`: *"And you, why are you depressed? What are you depressed about?"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`: *"Is the mongoose alive?"*).

Instructions are given bare, in the imperative, without softening — 9/10 samples (`chekhov-001-to-his-brother-mihail.txt`: *"Acquire the habit of doing so."*; `chekhov-154-to-his-sister.txt`: *"Write!"*). Only `chekhov-087-to-madame-avilov.txt` gives none.

The recipient is often addressed by an invented or inflated title rather than a name, and the joke is not explained — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: *"My dear Tunguses!"*; `chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin.txt`, dropped mid-sentence: *"and no wonder, your Excellency!"*).

## 5. Figures

An image arrives as an explicit comparison — *like*, *as though*, *such as* — to one specific creature, food, body or household object, never to a category — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: *"she eats mechanically, as though she were munching oats"*; *"its whistle is midway between the bray of an ass and an Aeolian harp"*; `chekhov-132-to-o-l-knipper.txt`: *"I am in the condition of a transplanted tree which is hesitating whether to take root or to begin to wither"*). Two samples carry no figure at all.

A figure runs one clause or one sentence and is not reopened; the paragraph moves on as if nothing had happened — 8/10 samples (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: the steam-tugs *"look like some fine young intellectual trying to run away while a plebeian wife, mother-in-law, sister-in-law, and wife's grandmother hold on to his coat-tails"*, after which the tugs are dropped).

## 6. What shifts, and what does not

The recipient changes the subject and the vocative and almost nothing else: the accumulating list and the deflating short sentence appear in letters to family and in letters to editors and strangers alike — 9/10 samples.

Under pressure to persuade, a frame gets repeated rather than varied — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s.txt`: *"Even if it were not so, even if for some reason I did not desire your visit, even then I should have received you"*; `chekhov-115-to-i-i-orlov.txt`: *"I don't believe in our educated class... I don't believe in it even when it's suffering"*). This does not partition by recipient — `chekhov-154-to-his-sister.txt` does it too, and it advises no one.

The physical report — legs, heels, appetite, sleep — is not reserved for intimates; it appears in the letter to an offended near-stranger as readily as in letters home — 5/10 samples (`chekhov-073-to-e-m-s.txt`: *"I am in bad health; for over a month I have had to keep indoors--influenza and cough."*).

At the point of maximum description, the writing announces its own inadequacy and keeps describing — 4/10 samples (`chekhov-043-to-a-s-suvorin.txt`: *"I am at a complete loss before them, and recognise my bankruptcy. How is one to describe them? ... Rocks, crags, forests..."*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried by short paragraphs, one to four sentences each, every one opening on its new subject named in the first clause — 10/10 samples (`chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`: *"Vesuvius hides its top in clouds"*, *"In Naples there is a magnificent arcade."*, *"I have been to a barber's"*). Nothing in the corpus breaks a piece up with a heading or a list, and no paragraph opens on a linking adverb — *however*, *moreover*, *in addition* — managing the turn.

The letter's shape is only visible from its end: subjects change when they change, and the close does not gather them up — 10/10 samples. Nothing in the corpus states in advance what the letter will cover, and nothing recaps it.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is an empty placeholder, so every observation above is derived from the corpus alone and none is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

The text is a translation. Idiom, punctuation and sentence joinery are partly Constance Garnett's, so no observation here about commas, semicolons, dashes, exclamation marks or capitalisation should be treated as the author's habit — only the grammatical and structural findings survive her — 10/10 samples.

The four-dot ellipsis is mostly the edition's, not the author's. Counted by position: ellipses that open or close a paragraph — an editor's cut mark — occur in 9/10 samples (`chekhov-087-to-madame-avilov.txt` opens *"... Yes, it is nice now in the country"*). Ellipses set mid-sentence between two lowercase words, where there is nothing to remove and the trailing-off is the author's, occur in only 3/10 (`chekhov-031-to-his-sister.txt`: *"Rain, cold, mud ... brrr!"*; `chekhov-056-to-his-sister.txt`: *"extremely like a fine ... den of thieves"*). Do not treat the ellipsis as a signature.

Every sample is a private letter to one named person the writer already knows, so nothing here shows how the voice behaves toward an audience it cannot name, or in any medium but the letter — 10/10 samples.

Only one sample predates 1888 and the rest span 1888–1904, so the corpus cannot show how the voice changed over a career; where a habit holds at 9/10 or 10/10 it is being read as constant, which the sampling cannot actually establish — 10/10 samples.

Twelve candidate observations were dropped for want of a citation or for holding in only one sample.
