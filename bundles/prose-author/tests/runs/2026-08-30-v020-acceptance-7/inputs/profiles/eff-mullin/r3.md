Use ordinary contractions in explanatory passages, transitions, and calls to action; allow the surrounding syntax and vocabulary—not formal expansion alone—to carry seriousness.

**Openings, paragraph endings, and closure.** Posts open with the bill, event, or institutional action in plain terms, often followed immediately by the central problem and its public-interest consequence. ## 5. Figures

**Figures and analogy vocabulary.** Figures are concrete and explanatory rather than ornamental: burdens become a “death sentence,” a database becomes a “vault,” or a legal disclaimer becomes a “smokescreen.” Comparisons translate technical or institutional mechanisms into familiar stakes. Use one vivid analogy near the consequence or takeaway, then return to literal policy language.

## 6. Register range

**Qualification and hedging.** Qualification usually concedes a legitimate goal or acknowledges uncertainty before identifying a concrete defect: a bill may sound reasonable, address some concerns, or pursue a laudable aim, yet remain unworkable or dangerous. Use brief concessions to establish fairness, then pivot to evidence and practical consequences.
