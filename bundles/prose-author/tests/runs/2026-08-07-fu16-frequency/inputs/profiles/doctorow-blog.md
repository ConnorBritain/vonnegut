# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

Ten usable samples, all carrying `source`, `date` and `human_authored: true`. Nothing was excluded. The source is a self-published blog, contemporary and untranslated, so no editor or compositor sits between the writer and the page; typographic observations need no caveat here. Each file wraps the composed essay in identical boilerplate (link roundups, appearance listings, a colophon) that recurs verbatim in all ten — every observation below is drawn from the essay only.

## 1. Cadence

Sentences accumulate — clause on clause, often carrying three or four examples — and are then stopped dead by a short flat declarative that passes judgement without qualifying it — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"DROP didn't get the memo."*). The instruction that follows: when a sentence has run past about forty words, make the next one under ten, and give it no hedge, no adverb and no subordinate clause.

The paragraph is the unit of evidence, and it ends on a colon with a bare URL on the next line — 10/10 samples, throughout (between five and seventeen times per piece; `2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"Austerity is fascism's best recruiting tool:"*). The colon is doing argumentative work, not typographic: the sentence before it is the claim, and the link is the receipt.

Three or more sentences built on one repeated frame, escalating — 8/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Give Trump everything he asks for and he'll demand more. Deny Trump anything and he'll demand more. Sign a contract with Trump and he'll break it."*). Build these in threes or fours and vary only the object.

The argument moves by asking the question the other side cannot answer, then answering it in the next sentence — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"if you can make all the money with your amazing system, why do you need my money?"*).

## 2. How a piece opens

Four shapes, checked across all ten. An opening happens once per piece, so no frequency applies.

A flat verdict on the subject in one short sentence — 3/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*). A deictic *"Here's…"* pointing at a fact or a borrowed sentence — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Here's a sentence that stopped me in my tracks last week"*). The opposition's doctrine or coinage set out neutrally and at length, so it can be taken apart afterwards — 4/10 samples (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"The theory of markets goes like this:"*). A personal anecdote — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"I first encountered that quote from someone extolling the virtues of bookstores"*). Pick one of the four; do not blend them.

## 3. How a piece closes

The last paragraph picks up the piece's own key term — usually a word owned by the people being argued against — and turns it around — 7/10 samples (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires"*). Two of the ten stop instead on a colon and a bare link rather than a sentence — 2/10 samples (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"the most exciting investment hypothesis is that 'hell is other people':"*).

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

A generic second person is put inside the mechanism being described, so the reader occupies the position the argument is about — 10/10 samples, throughout (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"So if you work five hours and don't wait on a single table, your boss has to pay you $36.25"*). Write the machinery in the second person rather than about a third party.

The author appears in the first person to mark an opinion or supply his own experience — 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"I think there's a whole cohort of investors who don't believe in AI"*).

Profanity and body-vulgarity land as verdicts rather than decoration — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"That's the whole fucking point of this exercise"*). One passage breaks the fourth wall to address a named target outside the readership — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Hey Khosrowshahi, the DOJ called and it wants its Clinton-era antitrust evidence back!"*).

Ratings are dropped into a noun phrase in parentheses rather than made into their own sentence — 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"America's (very, very weird) political system"*).

## 5. Figures

Images are made of bodies, animals, food, building materials and household objects, and then applied to something abstract — a law, a market, a policy — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"AI is the asbestos we're shoveling into the walls of our civilization"*). Reach for a physical act the reader has performed, not a concept.

One mundane transaction is extended across several consecutive paragraphs before being snapped back to the subject — 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper` runs an escrow agent for five paragraphs: *"Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed"*).

Another writer's coinage is borrowed and credited by name in the same breath — 9/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"John Gruber's term for"*). Opponents get a coined or repurposed collective noun rather than an adjective — 8/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"these Renfields clutch their pearls"*).

## 6. Register range

One register runs the corpus: argumentative exposition addressed to a readership already on side — 10/10 samples, throughout (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"Politics aren't about who rules – it's about whether we are ruled at all, or whether we are free."*). Two samples visibly step out of it, and each does so once. Where the subject is the author's own mortality, the mockery and the profanity drop away and the sentences shorten — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"He was only 44. I still miss him, every day"*). Where the piece is a how-to, it switches to second-person imperative with literal keystrokes — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Just hit ctrl-alt-r and see if the whole article appears."*). Beyond those two, the corpus is too narrow in subject and date to show whether the voice moves further.

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried inside the prose: where steps are needed they are written as sentences with their own paragraph breaks — 10/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Step one: Exist."* / *"Step two: There is no step two."*). No essay in the corpus uses a subheading or a bulleted list.

Where a claim is softened it is softened by owning it — the hedge is a first-person opinion marker — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"there's one area where I think Quiggin's got it wrong"*). No sample softens a claim impersonally: no *arguably*, *it could be argued*, *some might say* — 10/10 samples.

The last sentence is the argument at its sharpest and most compressed — 10/10 samples (`2026-07-10-posthuman-as-in-no-humans`: *"a massive convincer for any AI salesman who's hunting for would-be slavers to sell chatbots to."*). No piece in the corpus ends by restating what it just argued or by signing off.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is unfilled — *"Not filled in yet."* — so everything above is derived from the corpus alone and nothing here is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

All ten come from one blog inside a sixteen-day window in July 2026, at one length, on one cluster of subjects. Nothing here shows how the voice behaves at a different length, in a different medium, or across time — 10/10 samples.

All ten address a standing readership that already shares the author's premises. Apart from the single aside at Uber's CEO, nothing shows the voice writing for a hostile reader or a first-time one — 10/10 samples.

Every file carries the same boilerplate around the essay — *"You keep readin' em, I'll keep writin' 'em."* — which is template, not composed prose, and was not read as evidence — 10/10 samples.

The colon-and-bare-link move is inseparable from the fact that this is a linkblog with a publishing system behind it. A drafter writing a piece with no links to drop has nothing here telling them how the evidence would otherwise attach — 10/10 samples.

Seven candidate observations were dropped for want of a citation that survived checking.
