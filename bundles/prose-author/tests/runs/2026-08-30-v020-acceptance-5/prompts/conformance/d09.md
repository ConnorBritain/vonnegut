### Observation instructions

- o01 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:second-person-family]: Direct second-person language makes the argument conversational and places the reader inside the stakes, often turning an abstract system into an immediate encounter. - o02 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:first-person-plural-family]: First-person plural language builds a shared position between writer and returning reader, usually naming common exposure, responsibility, or possible action rather than a private group. - o03 [contraction-negation; section:cadence; measurement:contractions]: Contractions keep the argumentative voice brisk, spoken, and confrontational without sacrificing technical explanation. - o04 [contraction-negation; section:absences; measurement:uncontracted-negatives]: Expanded negative forms appear as occasional emphasis within otherwise compressed prose, giving a denial or correction extra weight. - o05 [profanity-vulgarity; section:register-range; measurement:profanity-vulgarity]: Profanity and vulgar phrasing function as deliberate rhetorical force: they puncture euphemism, expose hypocrisy, or make an institutional abuse feel materially offensive. - o06 [self-reference-biography; section:register-range; measurement:first-person-singular-family]: Singular self-reference presents the writer as an involved arguer, reader, and occasional witness whose experience supplies a bridge into the larger claim. - o07 [questions-imperatives-vocatives; section:address; measurement:question-marks]: Questions are used rhetorically to expose a contradiction, test an opponent’s premise, or lead the reader toward an answer that the surrounding argument supplies. - o08 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:round-parenthetical-spans]: Parenthetical asides interrupt the main line to add a qualification, joke, definition, attribution, or side observation without abandoning the argument. - o09 [interruption-punctuation; section:absences; measurement:em-dashes]: The corpus does not use em dashes as its interruption mark. - o10 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:en-dashes]: En dashes carry interruptions, appositional pivots, and compact qualifications while preserving forward motion. - o11 [qualification-hedging; section:register-range]: Qualification usually narrows a claim through concessions, explicit limits, counterexamples, and distinctions between what is possible and what is likely. - o13 [figures-analogy; section:figures]: Figures and analogies make systems legible by mapping them onto bodily risk, games, markets, household transactions, software, or familiar cultural scenes. - o14 [openings-endings-closure; section:openings]: Openings commonly begin with a striking sentence, quoted formulation, definition, irony, or concrete mechanism, then widen into the governing argument. Paragraphs often close on a compressed reversal or memorable image. Endings return to the central distinction or demand, leaving the reader with a sharpened conclusion rather than a neutral summary.
