- o01 [person-reader-stance; section:address; measurement:second-person-family]: Direct second-person language makes the argument conversational and places the reader inside the stakes, often turning an abstract system into an immediate encounter. - o05 [profanity-vulgarity; section:register-range; measurement:profanity-vulgarity]: Profanity and vulgar phrasing function as deliberate rhetorical force: they puncture euphemism, expose hypocrisy, or make an institutional abuse feel materially offensive. - o06 [self-reference-biography; section:register-range; measurement:first-person-singular-family]: Singular self-reference presents the writer as an involved arguer, reader, and occasional witness whose experience supplies a bridge into the larger claim. - o07 [questions-imperatives-vocatives; section:address; measurement:question-marks]: Questions are used rhetorically to expose a contradiction, test an opponent’s premise, or lead the reader toward an answer that the surrounding argument supplies. - o08 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:round-parenthetical-spans]: Parenthetical asides interrupt the main line to add a qualification, joke, definition, attribution, or side observation without abandoning the argument. Use them for compact tonal pivots and technical clarifications; let the surrounding sentence remain intelligible if the aside is removed.
- o09 [interruption-punctuation; section:absences; measurement:em-dashes]: The corpus does not use em dashes as its interruption mark. - o10 [interruption-punctuation; section:cadence; measurement:en-dashes]: En dashes carry interruptions, appositional pivots, and compact qualifications while preserving forward motion. - o11 [qualification-hedging; section:register-range]: Qualification usually narrows a claim through concessions, explicit limits, counterexamples, and distinctions between what is possible and what is likely. The prose often grants a partial point before reversing its implication. Use these concessions to make the attack more precise, not to soften its conclusion.
- o12 [opponents-allies-sources; section:register-range]: Sources enter as named writers, institutions, quoted formulations, linked evidence, or concepts that the argument extends and corrects. - o13 [figures-analogy; section:figures]: Figures and analogies make systems legible by mapping them onto bodily risk, games, markets, household transactions, software, or familiar cultural scenes. The comparison is commonly extended across several sentences until it exposes a distribution of power. Introduce the image early, develop its mechanism, and return to the literal claim.
- o14 [openings-endings-closure; section:openings]: Openings commonly begin with a striking sentence, quoted formulation, definition, irony, or concrete mechanism, then widen into the governing argument. Paragraphs often close on a compressed reversal or memorable image. Endings return to the central distinction or demand, leaving the reader with a sharpened conclusion rather than a neutral summary.
