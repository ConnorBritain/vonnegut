They soften institutional prose without making the argument casual. Use ordinary conversational contractions in explanatory and argumentative passages, while allowing surrounding legal or quoted language to retain its own formal register.

## 2. How a piece opens

**Openings, paragraph endings, and closure.** Openings commonly name the bill or practice, state its official purpose, and pivot quickly to the concrete harm or public-interest conflict. Sections then use descriptive headings and accumulate legal, institutional, and practical reasons before closing with a direct judgment or action. End by restating the remedy, rejecting the proposal, or directing readers toward participation.

It makes readers participants in a policy question, especially near explanations of how a law affects ordinary users. Use it selectively for stakes, examples, and calls to contact lawmakers, not as a continuous conversational mode.

## 5. Figures

**Figures and analogy vocabulary.** Figures are concrete and explanatory, not ornamental: AI training is compared to reading, a database is contrasted with checking IDs, and familiar institutional or technical examples make an abstract legal risk visible. Introduce the analogy after defining the policy mechanism, extend it only far enough to clarify stakes, and return quickly to the legal argument.

## 6. Register range

**Qualification and hedging.** Qualification is explicit but not timid: the prose distinguishes what a bill says, what it could require, and what companies or courts are likely to do. Concessions acknowledge improvements or legitimate aims before returning to the central objection. Place caveats immediately beside the claim they limit, then state the practical consequence.
