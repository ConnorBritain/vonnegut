# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

*All rates below are per 1,000 body words. The body of each post is the span between the first `(permalink)` line and the next section `(permalink)` line; bare-URL lines and the trailing `(Image: …)` credit are excluded from the word base, which is 17,438 words across the ten samples (17,549 with URL lines included). "Sentence unit" means a span produced by splitting on `.`, `!`, `?` or `:` — the colon counts as a terminator because this writer uses it as one.*

## 1. Cadence

The sentence is medium-length and the variation is wide. 788 sentence units, mean 22.1 words, median 21, running from one word to 88 — 10/10 samples, throughout (45.19 per 1,000 words). Paragraphs are short and even: 297 of them, mean 58.6 words, median 57 — 10/10 samples, throughout (17.03 per 1,000). Two or three sentences, then a break.

The variation is not decorative; it tracks the argument. Exposition accumulates in long units — 48 units of 45 words or more, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (2.75 per 1,000) — and the judgement lands in a very short one: 67 units of five words or fewer, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (3.84 per 1,000) (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Because we let them."*; `2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"This is a lie."*). **If a sentence has run past forty words explaining a mechanism, make the next one five words and flat.**

Three sentence-openers carry that alternation. `This` opens 46 units — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (2.64 per 1,000) — and it is the verdict move: a paragraph of mechanism is followed by a short sentence naming what it was (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"This is a transfer of risk from bosses to workers."*). `If` opens 42 units — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (2.41 per 1,000) — usually in stacked pairs that hold one variable and swap the other (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"If property rights are human rights, I can leave an apartment building empty while you freeze to death on its sidewalk."*). `But` opens 35 units — 9/10 samples, several times per piece (2.01 per 1,000) — and it is where a concession is withdrawn (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"But it doesn't have to be this way."*).

## 2. How a piece opens

The first sentence is short and already inside the subject: median 11.5 words, eight of the ten under 21 words, and no sample spends its opening sentence on what the piece is going to do — 10/10 samples (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers are a cancer."*; `2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"As a technology, AI isn't exceptional."*). No rate: there is one opening per piece, so the countable part is its length, given here.

I checked all ten and they fall into four shapes, not one: a flat verdict on the subject (3/10); a presentational colon that hands you the object (3/10 — `2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Here's an irony:"*); somebody else's words first, an epigraph or a borrowed coinage (2/10 — `2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"'I often wonder what the Vintners buy/One half so precious as the Goods they sell' -The Rubáiyát of Omar Khayyám"*); and a long concessive clause that grants the conventional reading before turning (2/10 — `2026-07-10-posthuman-as-in-no-humans`: *"While the AI bubble is primarily a material phenomenon (…), there is an inescapable ideological component to it"*). 10/10 samples fall into one of these four. **Do not average them.**

## 3. How a piece closes

The last sentence is a flat declarative judgement about what the named actor actually is or wants. There is no summary, no exhortation, no widening out — 10/10 samples (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"…they value their flexibility, which arises out of the constraints (technical, legal) they impose on us: the drivers and passengers."*).

In 4/10 samples the closing sentence contains, verbatim, a phrase of six characters or more that the same piece has earlier put in double quotes — 5 instances across the ten, once or twice per piece (0.29 per 1,000) (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"But the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires…"*). The rule is strict by design and undercounts the looser version of the move; 4/10 is what it supports.

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

The reader is *you*, constantly. 395 tokens of the you-family (`you`, `your`, `yours`, `yourself`, `you're`/`you've`/`you'll`/`you'd`), 10/10 samples, throughout (22.65 per 1,000). Alongside it, and much rarer, a *we* that stands with the reader rather than addressing them from outside: 107 tokens of the we-family (`we`, `us` lowercase only — uppercase `US` is the country — `our`, `ours`, `ourselves`, `we're`/`we've`/`we'll`/`we'd`), 10/10 samples, several times per piece (6.14 per 1,000) (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"even the best of us can fall prey to selfishness and rationalization"*). The writer appears as a person, not only as an arguer: 104 first-person-singular tokens (`I`, `I'm`/`I've`/`I'll`/`I'd`, `me`, `my`, `mine`, `myself`), 10/10 samples, several times per piece (5.96 per 1,000) (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"I'm 55."*).

The prose is contracted throughout: 193 instances counting `n't`, `'re`, `'ve`, `'ll`, `'d` and `'m` and excluding `'s` entirely, since `'s` cannot be separated from the possessive by rule — 10/10 samples, throughout (11.07 per 1,000).

The reader is told what to do, but rarely. 16 sentence units opening with a bare imperative from the stated list {Just, Stop, Click, Select, Take, Consider, Think, Remember, Note, Try, Use, Switch, Install, Load, Hover, Move, Add, Delete} — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece (0.92 per 1,000) (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Stop using Chrome, switch to Firefox"*). Profanity is reserved, not decorative: 19 instances from the stated list {shit, shits, shitty, shittiness, bullshit, fuck, fucks, fucking, asshole, assholes, piss, pissed, damned} — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece (1.09 per 1,000), and each lands on the sentence of maximum contempt (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"That's the whole fucking point of this exercise"*).

Two habits carry the argument's sourcing. First, the opponent's term is held at arm's length in short double quotes: 154 quoted spans of four words or fewer, 10/10 samples, throughout (8.83 per 1,000) (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"what Uber calls 'flexibility' is really a way for the company to offload its operational risks onto its drivers"*). Second, evidence arrives as a paragraph ending in a colon followed by a bare URL on its own line: 103 such pairs, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (5.91 per 1,000) (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"…for the same reason your dog licks its balls: because they can, and we don't stop them:"*). Of 112 colon-terminated paragraphs, 103 are followed by a link and 9 by a set-off block quotation.

Allies get named and quoted, and this is where the corpus is uneven. Counting a capitalised word in subject position immediately before a reporting verb from the fixed list {writes, wrote, says, said, notes, points out, puts it, calls, quipped, told, told me, likes to say, insists, argues, shows, cites, counsels}, allowing one intervening adverb: 27 instances in 6/10 samples, several times per piece within those (1.55 per 1,000) (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"As Weil points out"*). Four samples argue alone.

## 5. Figures

The manufactured compound is the unit. 186 hyphenated compounds (letter-hyphen-letter, URL lines excluded), 10/10 samples, throughout (10.67 per 1,000) — *ego-shattering*, *word-guessing*, *vagina-rocks*, *god-botherers*, *money-losingest*.

I cannot draw a line around a figure, so neither of the next two numbers is a count of figures; each is a count of the vocabulary a set of figures is built from, under a stated word list. Body and bodily function: 46 tokens from {orifice(s), asshole(s), piss, pissed, pee, bladder(s), kidney(s), vagina(s), vaginal, balls, throat(s), gut(s), mouth, face(s), heart(s), stomach, blood, bones, body, bodies, skin, hand(s), pocket(s), eyes, ear(s), pearls, hair, womb, foal, mare} — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (2.64 per 1,000) (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"if you emerged from a lucky orifice"*). Monarchy, piracy and slavery: 33 tokens from {king(s), queen, aristocracy, aristocrat(s), royal, royalty, feudal, lord(s), admiral(s), pirate(s), prince(s), throne, rents, rentier(s), dynasty, dynasties, monarch(s), serf(s), fiefdom, crown, sword, slave(s), slavery, slaver(s), master(s)} — 6/10 samples, several times per piece within those (1.89 per 1,000) (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"every pirate yearns to be an admiral"*).

The third source is a borrowed story, dropped in without setup and not explained — Omelas, Renfield, Les Miz, King Arthur's sword, the Leopard in the disused lavatory — 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece. **No rate**: I could not write a rule a stranger would apply the same way to decide what counts as a borrowed story, and a list I picked by ear is not a countable component.

## 6. Register range

The voice does move, and the movement is measurable. In the two samples that walk the reader through a procedure, the you-family rate roughly triples: 98 tokens in `2026-07-21-dickovers` and 78 in `2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`, against a range of 21–32 in the other eight — 10/10 samples checked. **When the piece turns into instructions, the second person takes over the paragraph.**

The first person moves the opposite way. `2026-07-14-designated-survivor`, the one sample that argues from the writer's own life, carries 29 first-person tokens against a median of 10 across the ten, and it is also the sample where the personal disclosure sits in a parenthesis rather than a sentence of its own — 10/10 samples checked (*"(my doctor just informed me that I am cancer free, following excision, radiotherapy and immunotherapy)"*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Interruption is done with a spaced en dash: 73 en dashes, 72 of them spaced, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (4.19 per 1,000) (`2026-07-09-wilhoitian`: *"The difference between the left and the right isn't the identities of the ruling class – it's whether we have a ruling class at all."*). Nothing in the corpus uses an em dash — 10/10 samples contain zero.

Qualification, self-correction and aside are done in round brackets: 97 parenthetical spans, 10/10 samples, several times per piece (5.56 per 1,000) (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Drivers get about 15 seconds (!) to decide whether to accept a job"*). Structure is carried entirely by short paragraphs and by the colon-then-link pair; nothing in the corpus breaks a piece up with a heading, a bulleted or numbered list, or a footnote — 10/10 samples.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is present but unfilled, so everything above is derived from the corpus alone and nothing here is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

The corpus is one author, one blog, and sixteen days in July 2026, addressed throughout to a returning readership that already knows the writer's terms — 10/10 samples. So nothing here shows how the voice behaves at another length, in another medium, or in front of an audience that has to be introduced to the argument from scratch. The you-rate spike in section 6 is measured across two samples out of ten and may be a property of the how-to subject rather than of a register the writer commands generally.

The figure-building move in section 5 has no rate, for the reason given there: the two word lists are components of it, not measurements of it, and a drafter should read them as raw material rather than as a target.

Four of the ten samples carry non-sentence blog furniture inside the body span — three an `(Image: …)` credit line, one a plug for the essay-formatted version — 4/10 samples. These are excluded from every count above and are not part of the voice.
