# Voice profile — doctorow-blog

Ten daily posts from one blog, 9–24 July 2026, all provenanced to the author's own site with no editor or translator in between — so the punctuation on the page is his and is reported here without caveat.

## 1. Cadence

The baseline sentence is long. Median sentence length runs 20–28 words in every sample, and every sample contains at least one sentence of 88 words or more — 10/10 samples, throughout. The long ones are built by accumulation: a list of instances or a chain of consequences bolted on with commas and dashes (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"They should understand that capitalists hate capitalism, that every pirate yearns to be an admiral."*).

Then the pile-up is stopped by a very short flat sentence that delivers the verdict on what just accumulated — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (once in the thinnest sample, nine times in the thickest): *"QED."*, *"It's bullshit."*, *"That's it. That's the crux."*, *"DROP didn't get the memo."*, *"I'm 55."* **If a sentence has run past forty words piling up evidence, make the next one under seven words and make it a judgement.**

Paragraphs are tiny: the median paragraph is exactly two sentences in every sample, and half to three-quarters of paragraphs are one or two — 10/10 samples, throughout.

The argument advances by conceding and reversing: give the other side its point in a clause, then take it back — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Sure, Canada's imposed some retaliatory tariffs on US products, but that's just a way of making everything Canadians buy more expensive"*). **When you are about to dismiss something, grant it first in half a sentence.**

## 2. How a piece opens

No piece sets a scene, gives a date and place, or warms up — the subject is on the page inside the first two sentences — 10/10 samples, once per piece.

Beyond that the ten openings are of four kinds, not one. Three open on a blunt short verdict (3/10 samples): *"Data brokers are a cancer."* Four open by stating the target's own theory or coinage straight, in its best form, before touching it (4/10 samples): *"The theory of markets goes like this: even the best of us can fall prey to selfishness and rationalization…"* Two open with a presentative *Here's a/an X:* hooking an outside quote (2/10 samples): *"Here's a sentence that stopped me in my tracks last week"*. One opens on an epigraph and a childhood memory — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-13-go-meta-meta`: *"As a larval hyperlexic, I just didn't understand what a bookseller could possibly buy with my money…"*). Do not blend these into a composite.

## 3. How a piece closes

The last paragraph picks up a word introduced in the opening third — usually the opponent's own term — and turns it against its owner — 8/10 samples, once per piece (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"the only 'billionaire derangement syndrome' that matters is the syndrome that affects billionaires"*). I checked all ten; the two that don't (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`, `2026-07-22-table-flipper`) close on a fresh formulation instead.

Two pieces end not on a sentence but on a colon and a bare URL — 2/10 samples.

## 4. Who is being addressed, and how

Everything is contracted — 10/10 samples, throughout. Across 17,600 words of body prose there are four uncontracted negations; two sit inside quoted material, and the other two are in one sample, both ventriloquising the opposition's belief (`2026-07-16-lucky-orifices`: *"people who are so perfect… that they do not need this kind of goad"*). **Uncontracted forms are available, but only for someone else's voice.**

The second person is a role-slot, not a greeting: *you* is where the reader gets stood inside the example as its victim or its perpetrator — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"if you work five hours and don't wait on a single table, your boss has to pay you $36.25"*).

Alongside that, an inclusive *we / us / our* that puts the writer on the reader's side of the transaction — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"the constraints (technical, legal) they impose on us: the drivers and passengers"*).

The writer appears as a person, not only as an arguer: his age, his cancer, his kid, his old job in a bookshop, what he already published — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"I'm 55. 20 years ago, it was unusual for just one of my peers to die in a given year"*).

Hedging is first-person and rationed to the speculative step: *I think*, *I'd add*, *I'm sure* — 6/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"I think billionaire solipsism can account for much of the malinvestment… but I don't think it's the whole story"*). The factual steps carry no softener.

Profanity is present in every sample and it lands on the verdict, not on the decoration — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Data brokers spy on us for the same reason your dog licks its balls"*; `2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"It's bullshit."*).

The opposition's vocabulary is quarantined in quotation marks every time it is used — *"flexibility"*, *"revealed preferences"*, *"legitimate interest"*, *"AI safety"* — 9/10 samples, several times per piece. And the opposition is named: at least one living person, by surname, in the body — 9/10 samples, several times per piece. Beside the names sits a coined or repurposed collective epithet for the group — *think-tankies*, *chuds*, *normies*, *Renfields*, *AI god-botherers* — 10/10 samples, once or twice per piece.

A mid-sentence parenthesis carries the judgement or the joke that would otherwise stop the sentence — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-23-drop-a-dime`: *"Once you complete this (pointless, redundant) step"*; `2026-07-11-your-risk`: *"Drivers get about 15 seconds (!) to decide"*).

## 5. Figures

The load-bearing figure is a mundane transaction explained patiently at length and then broken: the escrow agent, the tipped restaurant server, the market stall where the stallholder reaches into your pocket, the murder-offset, the beauty contest — 7/10 samples, once or twice per piece (`2026-07-22-table-flipper`: *"Trump is the escrow agent who keeps the money and the deed, then announces he did it because the seller was a fentanyl dealer and the buyer was a lizard-person"*).

The short figures come from the body and what it does — orifices, bladders, piss, kidneys, balls, a face being punched — 10/10 samples, several times per piece (`2026-07-24-supplemental-income`: *"just a way to give yourself very expensive piss"*).

A named person's verbatim words are the hinge the argument turns on, quoted at length and then answered in the writer's own voice — 8/10 samples, once or twice per piece (Brust, Weil, Quiggin, Gruber, Sinclair, Keynes, Farrell, Thiel).

## 6. Register range

The mechanics do not move across a browser how-to, a mortality essay and a geopolitical analysis: same contraction rate, same two-sentence paragraph, same colon-and-link rhythm, same profanity on the verdict — 10/10 samples.

Two shifts are visible, each in one place. The how-to switches to imperatives and literal keystrokes — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-21-dickovers`: *"Just hit ctrl-alt-r and see if the whole article appears."*). The mortality essay drops to one profanity and hands the short sentences to biography — 1/10 samples, the only place this appears (`2026-07-14-designated-survivor`: *"He was only 44. I still miss him, every day"*).

## 7. What the corpus never does

Structure is carried entirely by the evidence hand-off: a paragraph makes its claim, ends on a colon, and the next line is a bare URL — 10/10 samples, throughout, four to fourteen times per piece. Nothing in the corpus breaks a piece up with a heading, a bulleted list or a numbered list.

Attribution is always to a named party quoted verbatim — 10/10 samples. Nothing in the author's own sentences attributes a view to *some argue*, *critics say*, *arguably*, or *it could be argued*; the single *Furthermore* in the corpus sits inside a block quotation of someone else.

The close reuses the opponent's own word rather than summarising — 10/10 samples. Nothing in the corpus ends with *in conclusion*, *to sum up*, or a stated takeaway.

## 8. What this profile could not determine

`voice.md` is unfilled, so every observation here is derived from the corpus alone and none of it is corroborated by the author's own account — 10/10 samples.

All ten samples are daily posts from one blog inside a sixteen-day window, one medium and one returning audience — so nothing here shows how the voice behaves in a book, a speech, fiction, or a direct reply to a hostile reader — 10/10 samples. The same narrow window means the typographic habits (spaced en dash, scare quotes, parenthetical asides) are established only for July 2026 and not shown to be stable over years — 10/10 samples.

Every sample is an attack on something. Praise, where it occurs, is compressed into a parenthesis or a single adjective — *"Zitron's (excellent) point"*, *"deliciously ingenious"* — so nothing in the corpus shows what this voice does when its subject is something it approves of at length — 10/10 samples.
