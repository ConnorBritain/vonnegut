**Category status**

1. **Register breaks** — clean. The draft runs the same two-register alternation the corpus
   runs everywhere: an elevated, faintly mock-formal narrator ("chilly gusts of wind with a
   taste of rain in them had well nigh de-peopled the streets", line 3-4; "to cast his watchful
   eye adown the pacific thoroughfare", lines 7-8) set against unbuttoned American speech in
   the dialogue ("Bully; it has given me everything I asked it for", line 105). Sample-06 does
   this across the same span (the Bogle's cruets paragraph against "Gee! but I slapped him a
   good one"); sample-05 does it between the narrator's opening and "It's a mighty little old
   world." No paragraph here could be lifted into a different document.

2. **Constructions the author does not use** — clean. Every shape in the draft has corpus
   parallels: the participial pile-up before the subject ("Trying doors as he went, twirling
   his club with many intricate and artful movements, turning now and then to...", lines 6-8)
   matches sample-06's "At the cashier's desk sits Bogle, cold, sordid, slow, smouldering, and
   takes your money" and sample-09's "he sat, comely, smooth-faced, hard, indomitable,
   good-natured, upon a stool." The delayed-apposition sentence at lines 85-88 ("And in the
   door of the hardware store the man who had come a thousand miles to fill an appointment,
   uncertain almost to absurdity, with the friend of his youth, smoked his cigar and waited")
   is the same held-breath construction as sample-08's "Which instigates the moral reflection
   that life is made up of sobs, sniffles, and smiles." The closing device — a plain document
   handed over and quoted to carry the reversal (lines 142-147) — appears verbatim in kind in
   sample-03 (Robert Ashburton's letter, lines 129-138), and the last-line reveal appears in
   sample-01, sample-09 and sample-10.

3. **Vocabulary reaching outside the corpus** — clean. "De-peopled", "pacific thoroughfare",
   "stanchest", "egotism enlarged by success" sit inside the corpus's habit of dropping a
   Latinate or archaic word into plain narration: "contumelious nostril" (sample-04),
   "corniform, be-spectacled nose" and "insoluble in the menstruum of his backwardness"

   in range: "You bet!", "put a razor-edge on him", "'Silky' Bob" against sample-07's "Pipe

4. **Rhythm** — no scan supplied; I will not guess at a measured verdict. On reading, the

   Nothing reads as flattened, but this is an impression, not a measurement.

   is the same deliberate withdrawal of the narrator seen at the end of sample-09 and in
   sample-03's letter.

gentle reader", sample-05; "let us discreetly face the other way and gossip", sample-02) and

markedly shorter and more compressed than every sample; length is not a finding.
