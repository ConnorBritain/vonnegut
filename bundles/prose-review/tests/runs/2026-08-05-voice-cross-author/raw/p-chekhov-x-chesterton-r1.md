## Findings

**LOCATION** — draft.txt, whole document; representative span lines 24-27:
"All the beasts of the field are respectable; it is only man who has broken loose. All
animals are domestic animals; only man is ever undomestic. All animals are tame animals;
it is only we who are wild."

**WHAT** — The draft is pitched as sustained impersonal argument, where the corpus is
occasional, first-person, and anchored to a person the writer is addressing; this is not a
paragraph that could be lifted out, it is the entire document sitting at a register the
corpus never occupies.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE** — Every one of the ten samples is addressed to somebody and stays
inside a private occasion: "DEAR BROTHER MISHA, I got your letter when I was fearfully
bored and was sitting at the gate yawning" (sample-01, lines 5-8); "MY DEAR TUNGUSES! I am
floating on the Kama, but I can't fix the exact locality" (sample-03, lines 85-87); "And
you, why are you depressed? What are you depressed about?" (sample-09, lines 15-16). When
the author does generalise, it is one movement inside a letter and it is tethered to named
persons and to the correspondent's own complaint — sample-08 lines 11-19: "Not the tutor
but the whole educated class--that is to blame, my dear sir... Remember that Katkov,
Pobyedonostsev, Vishnegradsky, were nurselings of the Universities." The draft has no
addressee, no occasion, and no such tether; the generalisation is the whole of it.

**CONFIDENCE** — high

**LOCATION** — draft.txt, lines 62-68: "And if any one asks, 'Why did the people of these
flat countries instinctively raise these riotous and towering monuments?' the only answer
one can give is, 'Because they were the people of these flat countries.' If any one asks,
'Why the men of Bruges sacrificed architecture and everything to the sense of dizzy and
divine heights?' we can only answer, 'Because Nature gave them no encouragement to do so.'"

**WHAT** — A staged objection-and-reply in which the writer invents a questioner, quotes
him, and quotes his own answer back — twice in parallel; this shape is absent from all ten
samples.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE** — The author asks rhetorical questions constantly but never supplies a
manufactured interlocutor or a quoted answer; he asks and then abandons the question, or
answers it with a fact. Sample-04, lines 9-11: "I am quite incapable of describing anything
so beautiful as the shores of the Amur; I am at a complete loss before them, and recognise
my bankruptcy. How is one to describe them? ... Rocks, crags, forests, thousands of ducks"
— question, then an unframed list, no "the only answer one can give is." Sample-02, line 10:
"But, Allah kerim, why do they both specialize?" is left standing. Sample-03, lines 194-195:
"Now isn't it revolting? Isn't it disgusting?" — no answer offered at all. Quotation marks
in this corpus enclose things people actually said (sample-03 line 227, the relation who
lives in a "substantial way"; sample-05 line 39, the fiancée's "that horrid woman"), never a
hypothetical position being set up for reply.

**CONFIDENCE** — high

**LOCATION** — draft.txt, lines 24-27 (quoted above) and lines 6-13: "But this sort of
sublime deformity is characteristic of the whole fancy and energy of these Flemish cities.
Flanders has the flattest and most prosaic landscapes, but the most violent and extravagant
of buildings. Here Nature is tame; it is civilisation that is untamable."

**WHAT** — Triadic anaphora — the same clause frame restated three times with the terms
rotated, each restatement raising the pitch rather than adding information — appears
repeatedly in the draft and in none of the ten samples.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE** — The author's repetitions are accumulative and flat, listing unlike
things rather than reworking one proposition: "All the journey these gentlemen have been
occupied in eating, settling mighty questions and eating, reading and eating" (sample-03,
lines 178-179); "The cabs are something inconceivable, wretched, dirty, drenched, without
springs" (sample-03, line 198); "science is advancing and advancing, social
self-consciousness is growing, moral questions begin to take an uneasy character, and so on,
and so on" (sample-08, lines 28-29). The nearest thing to the draft's shape in the whole
corpus is sample-08 lines 21-22, "I don't believe in it even when it's suffering... I believe
in individual people" — two beats, inside a letter, not three parallel aphorisms in a row.

**CONFIDENCE** — high

**LOCATION** — draft.txt, line 24: "This savagery even in stone is the expression of the
special spirit in humanity"; line 30-33: "it arose in the most equably civilised period the
world has ever seen"; line 35-36: "something in it that breaks the outline of perfect and
conventional beauty."

**WHAT** — Abstract nouns are the grammatical subjects doing the work ("this savagery,"
"the expression of the special spirit in humanity," "conventional beauty"), a level of
abstraction above anything in the corpus, where the subjects of sentences are people,
weather, objects, and the writer himself.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE** — The author reaches for a concrete substitute even when the thought is
general: instead of an abstraction about tedious moralising he writes "an unpleasant
sensation which mortals feel after eating too many raisins or currants" (sample-01, lines
22-23); instead of an abstraction about the Kama's dullness, "one would think the
inhabitants were employed in the manufacture of clouds, boredom, soaking fences and mud in
the streets" (sample-03, lines 162-164); instead of an abstraction about his own displacement,
"I am in the condition of a transplanted tree which is hesitating whether to take root or to
begin to wither" (sample-09, lines 17-19). Where he does use an abstract subject it is plain
and short — "Life is hard in this world!" (sample-02, line 44) — not "the expression of the
special spirit in humanity."

**CONFIDENCE** — high

**LOCATION** — draft.txt, lines 84-96: "The Spaniards (it was said) are passionate because
their country is hot... Thus Spaniards have discovered more continents than Scandinavians
because their hot climate discouraged them from exertion. Thus Dutchmen have fought for
their freedom quite as bravely as Switzers because the Dutch have no mountains."

**WHAT** — A built argumentative apparatus: a stated opposing thesis, an announced
counter-list, "Thus" as a serial inferential connective, and a concession managing the
reader's objection — machinery absent from all ten samples.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE** — The author's opinions arrive undefended and unstaged, as assertion
plus example: "I don't believe in our educated class, which is hypocritical, false,
hysterical, badly educated and indolent" (sample-08, lines 19-21) is the corpus's most
argumentative passage and it builds no counter-list and uses no inferential connective. His
transitions are the plain ones — "But," "And," "Though," "That's not true, though"
(sample-03, line 127) — and his summary of a position he rejects is one clause long: "This
is not specialization but partiality" (sample-02, lines 14-15).

**CONFIDENCE** — high
