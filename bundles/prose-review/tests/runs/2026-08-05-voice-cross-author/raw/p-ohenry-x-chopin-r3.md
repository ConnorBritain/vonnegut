# Voice review — draft.txt against corpus (10 samples)

No voice card supplied; findings are drawn from the ten corpus samples directly. No
deterministic rhythm scan was supplied, so category 4 is assessed by reading only and is
reported as such below.

The findings below are not localised to a passage. The draft differs from the corpus in the
same way at every point in it, so the spans cited are representative rather than exhaustive.

---

## 1. Register break — whole document

**LOCATION**: lines 52–58 —
"He never assumed this seriocomic tone when alone with Mrs. Pontellier. She never knew
precisely what to make of it; at that moment it was impossible for her to guess how much of
it was jest and what proportion was earnest. It was understood that he had often spoken words
of love to Madame Ratignolle, without any thought of being taken seriously."

**WHAT**: The narrator reports a character's inner uncertainty straight, from inside her, and
lets it stand — the corpus narrator never holds a sincere interior close-up without breaking
it with a joke, an aside, or a turn to the reader.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE**: The author writes interiority under comic pressure every time. sample-09
lines 30–32: "She tinctured all his thoughts; she was the compound extract of all that was
chemically pure and officinal... his hopes remained insoluble in the menstruum of his
backwardness and fears." sample-06 lines 105–107 reaches the same emotional register the
draft occupies and then names it as a general truth in the author's own comic idiom: "But deep
below our freckles and hay-coloured hair the unhandsomest of us dream of a prince or a
princess." sample-02 line 116 does the identical thing with Dulcie's taste in art: "no critic
had elevated his eyebrows at her infantile entomologist." The draft's paragraph could be
lifted out and dropped into a different document without leaving a hole.

**CONFIDENCE**: high

## 2. Construction absent from the corpus — direct address to the reader

**LOCATION**: absent throughout, lines 1–139. Nearest occasions where the corpus would use it:
line 8 "He had lived in her shadow during the past month. No one thought anything of it";
lines 94–96 "The youngsters came tumbling up the steps... Mrs. Pontellier made them carry her
paints and things into the house."

**WHAT**: The draft has no second-person address, no "let us", no narratorial aside to the
reader, and no acknowledgement that a story is being told — in a document of this length the
corpus would have several.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE**: Present in all ten samples, usually more than once each, and used exactly
at the scene-transition and describe-the-room junctures the draft passes over silently.
sample-08 lines 13–14: "While the mistress of the home is gradually subsiding from the first
stage to the second, take a look at the home." sample-08 lines 164–165: "For ten seconds let
us regard with discreet scrutiny some inconsequential object in the other direction."
sample-02 lines 117–118: "While she swiftly makes ready, let us discreetly face the other way
and gossip." sample-02 lines 48–49: "Don't care? Very well; probably you are interested in
larger amounts." sample-05 lines 23–24: "Say, gentle reader, did you ever have a 200-pound
woman..." sample-06 line 44: "Will it tire you to be told again that Aileen was beautiful?"
sample-06 line 34: "Why do you suggest Matilda? Please listen this time." sample-01 line 10:
"I invoke your consideration of the scene." sample-04 lines 237–238: "The story should end
here. I wish it would as heartily as you who read it wish it did." sample-03 opens on a park
bench, the same setup as the draft, and still reaches for the reader's judgement at lines
21–22: "It was a good handkerchief, a good brow, and the young man was good to look at."
sample-07 lines 206–207: "This movement on the part of the Clover Leaf members requires a word
of elucidation."

**CONFIDENCE**: high

## 3. Construction absent from the corpus — no vernacular in dialogue

**LOCATION**: lines 25–29, 31–36, 126–134 — all spoken lines in the draft, e.g. "Oh, come!"
he insisted. "You mustn't miss your bath. Come on. The water must be delicious; it will not
hurt you. Come."

**WHAT**: Every character in the draft speaks the same educated standard English as the
narrator; the corpus puts a phonetically rendered or slangy speech register against the
narrator's in all ten samples.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE**: sample-03 lines 43–47 ("dat gent on de oder bench sent yer a song and
dance by me"); sample-06 lines 113–116 ("Sashayed up, so he did, and made a break. I turned
him down, cold"); sample-07 lines 62–63 ("Oh, gee! Mag ain't made a hit—oh, no!"); sample-10
lines 22–24 ("'Tis here, I will be diverted"); sample-09 lines 62–65 ("Not any Dagoes... Rosy
and me are goin' to run away"); sample-04 lines 46–47 ("Money'll do it as slick as soap
grease"); sample-01 lines 202–203 ("and he wouldn't stand for no knockin' the place");
sample-02 lines 58–62 ("Well, ain't you the lucky one? Piggy's an awful swell"); sample-05
lines 130–132 ("darn my eyes if the saffron-coloured son of a seltzer lemonade ain't asking me
in"); sample-08 lines 79–80 ("Take yer hat off and let's have a sight at the looks of it").
Ten of ten. Even sample-03's cultivated young lady is placed opposite a boy who says "I'm shy
on one word, lady."

**CONFIDENCE**: high

## 4. Construction absent from the corpus — whole sentences of untranslated French dialogue

**LOCATION**: lines 35–36 "'_Passez! Adieu! Allez vous-en!_'"; line 50
"'_Blagueur—farceur—gros bête, va!_'"; line 78 "'_Mais ce n'est pas mal! Elle s'y connait,
elle a de la force, oui._'"; line 31 "_Par exemple!_"

**WHAT**: Complete utterances are given in French and left unglossed, carrying dialogue the
reader is expected to follow.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE**: The corpus uses foreign words, but only as isolated nouns inside English
syntax, and typically as the setup for a joke. sample-01 lines 13–15: "the sedulous and
largess-loving _garçons_... the _mélange_ of talk and laughter"; sample-01 line 26 "a _table
d'hôte_ grape fruit"; and sample-01 lines 192–193 gets a punchline out of it — "I called
McCarthy, one of the French _garçons_." sample-02 line 54 does the same with Latin: "sticking
her hat-pin within an eighth of an inch of her _medulla oblongata_." sample-06 line 8 "that
highway of _bourgeoisie_." No sample carries a line of dialogue in a language other than
English. Note that the italicised markup itself is not the finding — the finding is that
untranslated French does the speaking, which survives being read aloud.

**CONFIDENCE**: high

## 5. Vocabulary reaching outside the corpus

**LOCATION**: line 6 "_camaraderie_"; line 20 "vouchsafe"; line 38 "interjoined"; line 52
"seriocomic"; line 74 "ejaculatory expressions of appreciation"; line 104 "languorous"; line
105 "befurbelowed"; line 131 "whose sonorous murmur reached her like a loving but imperative
entreaty."

**WHAT**: Ornate diction used at full sincerity on a subject that matches its formality.

This is deliberately *not* a finding about ornate vocabulary as such — the corpus is at least
as Latinate, and formal register alone is not evidence. The difference is what the high
diction is aimed at. In the corpus it is always cocked against a low subject and detonates:
"pulchritude" is applied to a waitress so that men "might season and make ambrosia of their
bacon and eggs" (sample-06 lines 96–99); "the air of conscious conviction of the
supereminence of the male in the cosmogony of creation" describes men flexing at a paper-box
factory dance (sample-07 lines 83–85); "a safe terpsichorean performance" describes a
tightrope walk over Niagara (sample-07 lines 139–141); "Ikey's corniform, be-spectacled nose"
(sample-09 line 22); "the counterfeit of that benign sauce" (sample-06 line 16). Every ornate
word in the corpus is a joke about the gap between the word and the thing. Not one of the
draft's ornate words has a low target: "sonorous murmur... like a loving but imperative
entreaty" is aimed at the sea, and means it.

**CONFIDENCE**: high

## 6. Voice that never shifts

**LOCATION**: whole document, lines 1–139.

**WHAT**: The draft holds one tone — measured, distant, unbroken — from the opening tableau to
the closing walk to the beach. The corpus visibly shifts, and shifts *within* a single piece,
not only between pieces.

**CORPUS EVIDENCE**: The corpus shift is documented, which is the condition for raising this
category. sample-02 changes register three times inside one story: a mock-theological
burlesque (lines 1–14), a flat ledger of a shop-girl's weekly expenses (lines 120–130), and a
sincere close on a girl crying over a photograph (lines 195–207). sample-08 moves from the
narrator's arithmetic joke about $1.87 to the unguarded "Please God, make him think I am still
pretty" (line 125) and back out to the magi peroration. sample-06 runs comic catalogue
(Bogle's cruets, lines 11–15) straight into "sobbing her heart out—out and back again to the
grey plain" (lines 219–221). sample-05 is a dog's wisecrack throughout; sample-03 is nearly
all dialogue in two clashing dictions. The draft's opening sentence and its closing sentence
are pitched identically.

**CONFIDENCE**: high

4. **Rhythm that flattens** — **not assessed.** No deterministic rhythm scan was supplied. By

   category.
5. **Voice that never shifts** — not clean (finding 6).

No finding here rests on formatting, punctuation, heading style, or markup. The draft is not
worse-written than the corpus and its subject matter being new is not among the reasons cited;
every finding above is a difference that survives being read aloud.
