# Voice review — case-10

No voice card supplied; corpus present (10 samples), so this is not a stop condition. No
deterministic rhythm scan supplied — category 4 is reported as unassessable rather than guessed.

The draft does not diverge from the corpus in one passage. It diverges throughout, and the
whole document is one span. Findings below cite representative spans; the scope of each is the
full draft.

---

**FINDING 1 — Register break (whole document)**

- **LOCATION**: lines 1–5, "I have been standing where everybody has stood, opposite the great / Belfry Tower of Bruges, and thinking, as every one has thought (though / not, perhaps, said), that it is built in defiance of all decencies of / architecture." Same register holds to line 111.
- **WHAT**: The draft is a contemplative first-person travel essay in a genteel literary register — suspended periodic sentences, impersonal "one," an unhurried address to a general reader — pitched at a formality and a distance the corpus never occupies.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: All ten samples open the argument flat-footed and concrete, at conversational distance and in the present tense of a specific grievance: "Data brokers are a cancer." (sample-05, line 21); "Here's an irony: the 'gig economy' is a statistical black hole." (sample-02, line 21); "It may strike you as weird, but lenders love to lend money to poor people who will have trouble paying back their loans." (sample-06, line 21); "The greatest magic trick of them all is lying." (sample-09, line 21); "Forget 'Don't be evil'; Google's true motto is a form of vulgar spidermanism" (sample-10, line 21). The author's essays are argumentative and cited, built on a chain of external sources; the draft is a reverie with no external reference of any kind. The register difference survives reading aloud: the corpus voice is loud, profane and hurried, the draft's is quiet and ruminative.
- **CONFIDENCE**: high

**FINDING 2 — Construction absent from the corpus: the staged interlocutor answered by tautology**

- **LOCATION**: lines 62–68, "And if any one asks, 'Why did the people of these flat / countries instinctively raise these riotous and towering monuments?' the / only answer one can give is, 'Because they were the people of these / flat countries.'" Repeated immediately at lines 65–68.
- **WHAT**: A rhetorical question is put into the mouth of an imagined generic questioner and answered with a deliberate circularity offered as wisdom — a paradox-shaped closing move.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: Absent from all ten samples. The author asks rhetorical questions constantly, but answers them with a fact, a mechanism, or a joke, never with a paradox: "Why do data brokers exist? Because we let them." (sample-05, line 25); "What's a legit interest? Well, I can tell you what it isn't." (sample-04, line 37); "Why loan $1m to Elon Musk at 0.5% interest when you can make 10,000 $100 payday loans to non-union Tesla workers on food stamps at 1,000% interest?" (sample-06, line 25); "What's a labor organizer to do?" answered by the Syndicom pizza tactic (sample-02, lines 91–93). The corpus also uses a two-beat catechism — "Step one: Exist. / Step two: There is no step two." (sample-05, lines 89–91) — which is a punchline, not a paradox.
- **CONFIDENCE**: high

**FINDING 3 — Construction absent from the corpus: the impersonal "one" and the suspended qualifier**

- **LOCATION**: line 64, "the only answer one can give"; line 96, "I could extend the list for ever. But however long it was"; lines 104–107, "As it is therefore quite doubtful whether a person will go specially with his environment or specially against his environment, I cannot comfort myself with the thought that…"; lines 2–4, "as every one has thought (though not, perhaps, said)".
- **WHAT**: Impersonal "one" as the subject of the argument, plus long front-loaded subordinate clauses that suspend the main verb to the end of the sentence.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: Absent from all ten samples. The author is relentlessly first- and second-person and puts the verb early: "I collect definitions of 'right' and 'left.'" (sample-01, line 41); "I'm 55." (sample-03, line 41); "Look, I know I'm no mathematician" (sample-07, line 103); "Let's be clear: I'm not giving you permission to pirate my work." (sample-08, line 97); "I don't know why Google doesn't do this." (sample-10, line 89). The one place the corpus uses "one wonders" it is plainly ironic and immediately abandoned for "you" (sample-05, line 51).
- **CONFIDENCE**: high

**FINDING 4 — Vocabulary reaching outside the corpus (abstract-literary register)**

- **LOCATION**: line 6, "sublime deformity"; line 18, "like monsters from the primal mire"; lines 32–33, "the most equably civilised period the world has ever seen"; line 55, "this furious fancy"; line 63, "these riotous and towering monuments"; line 102, "desired to exalt their towers till they struck the stars."
- **WHAT**: A sustained diction of abstract aesthetic elevation — "sublime," "primal," "equably," "fancy," "exalt" — from a different register than the author's, which is concrete, technical and vernacular.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: The corpus reaches for the vivid rather than the elevated, and grounds every abstraction in a mechanism or an obscenity: "unsecured burrito mortgages" (sample-06, line 29); "a demented hairball of form-filling" (sample-05, line 107); "digital arm-breakers" (sample-06, line 81); "you can tax the shit out of empty homes" (sample-01, line 69); "It's bananas." (sample-07, line 103); "a sphincter-puckering legal letter" (sample-08, line 91). Where the corpus does go literary it borrows a pop or genre reference — "breeding horses to run faster and faster until one of them foals a locomotive" (sample-09, line 33) — not a Latinate abstraction. This is not a rare-word finding; it is a level-of-abstraction finding sustained across the whole draft.
- **CONFIDENCE**: high

**FINDING 5 — Vocabulary reaching outside the corpus (racial epithet)**

- **LOCATION**: line 39, "there is in it a touch of the n—— hymn" (draft prints the slur in full).
- **WHAT**: A racial slur used unremarked, as ordinary descriptive vocabulary.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: Absent from all ten samples, and contrary to the author's consistent handling of race, which names racism as racism and stands against it: "the racist practice of 'redlining' (denying government-backed loans to Black borrowers)" (sample-06, line 53); "the right's obsession with learning the racial and gender markers of anyone involved in a plane crash" (sample-01, line 45); "murdering brown people in gulags" (sample-01, line 29); "North Carolina's voter suppression law struck down as 'racist'" (sample-07, line 143); "Stories from Black women's customer service hell" (sample-07, line 157). No sample uses a slur in the author's own voice, quoted or otherwise.
- **CONFIDENCE**: high

**FINDING 6 — Voice that never shifts**

- **LOCATION**: whole draft, lines 1–111.
- **WHAT**: The draft holds a single meditative tone from the first line to the last, including across its two section breaks (lines 22, 70), where the subject moves from architecture to child-rearing to national character without any change in pitch.
- **CORPUS EVIDENCE**: The corpus visibly shifts with subject matter. Sample-03 moves from political snark ("the Mad King," line 27) to plain elegy ("15 years ago, one of my dearest friends, a contemporary, went to bed in seeming perfect health and never woke up… I still miss him, every day", line 71) inside one essay. Sample-04 drops from polemic into flat procedural instruction ("Just click the little 'page view' icon next to the Firefox location bar or use the key combo 'ctrl-alt-r.'", line 57). Sample-08 ends on two three-word paragraphs ("Life is hard. / Read books.", lines 113–115). Sample-02 breaks its own frame for an aside in a different voice entirely ("holy shit, is Uber's investor relations site seriously serving ASPX pages in 2026?!", line 69). The draft has no such gear change.
- **CONFIDENCE**: high
