# Five things to do before you calibrate

Calibration is the step that turns the scanner from a guess into a measurement of your own writing. It is also the step everyone skips, because the corpus is never ready. Here is what makes it ready.

## The list

Gather ten pieces you wrote unaided, in one register. Fewer than five and the calibrator refuses; fewer than ten and it calls the bands thin. Ten is where confidence starts.

Strip anything a tool wrote for you. A paragraph a model drafted and you lightly edited belongs in the approved folder, not the human one, and the ingest tool computes how much of it is yours rather than asking.

Attach provenance to every file: where it came from, when you wrote it, and your own attestation that no model touched it. Samples without it are excluded and listed by name.

Keep registers apart. An essay corpus with three emails in it produces bands that describe neither, wide enough that every draft lands inside them and the tool goes quiet while looking as if it works.

Commit the corpus on its own. A sample that arrives as a side effect of some other session is exactly the one nobody checks, and a wrongly attested sample drags the thresholds toward machine norms with nothing in the output to show it.

## After that

Run the calibrator with the write flag, read the thin-corpus warning if it prints one, and lock what you like before it drifts.
