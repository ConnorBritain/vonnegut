# Chapter Three

The rain had stopped by the time Mara reached the bridge, but the river had not noticed. It ran brown and fast under the arches, carrying a door, a chair, and something she decided not to look at twice. She had promised Teodor she would be at the mill by noon. It was past two.

The mill was where she had left it, which was the first surprise of the day. The second was that Teodor was not. His coat hung on the nail by the door, still wet. The ledger lay open on the bench, and the last line was in a hand she did not know.

***

Teodor had never been late in the eleven years she had known him. He had never been early either; he arrived, and things began. So the empty mill was not an absence. It was a message, and she did not yet know the language.

She read the ledger line again. Forty sacks, it said, delivered to the Harrow road. There was no Harrow road. There had been one, before the flood of 1898 took the bridge and the village with it, and nobody had called it that since.

***

By dusk she had decided. She took the coat, because it was his and because it was cold, and she walked toward the place where the Harrow road used to be. Behind her the river went on carrying things it had no use for.
