   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The year he left school, which every later "some years later" and "not long after" in the revision depends on.

4. **CLASS**: dropped-fact
   **ORIGINAL**: L14–15 "In 1888 I gained the Pushkin prize."
   **REVISION**: L14–15 "I won a literary prize not long after"
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: Both the year and which prize it was. The Pushkin prize is now an unnamed "literary prize", and "not long after" hides a four-year gap.

5. **CLASS**: dropped-fact
   **ORIGINAL**: L15–16 "In 1890 I made a journey to Sahalin across Siberia and back by sea."
   **REVISION**: L15–16 "travelled to Sahalin across Siberia and back by sea"
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The year of the Sahalin journey. With it goes the anchor for "the following year", so the Europe tour can't be dated either.

6. **CLASS**: dropped-fact
   **ORIGINAL**: L16–17 "In 1891 I made a tour in Europe, where I drank excellent wine and ate oysters."
   **REVISION**: L16–17 "and the following year made a tour in Europe"
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The absolute year of the Europe tour. "The following year" points only to a journey that no longer has a year.

7. **CLASS**: claim-drift
   **ORIGINAL**: L17–18 "In 1892 I took part in an orgy in the company of V. A. Tihonov at a name-day party."
   **REVISION**: L17–18 "Last year I took part in an orgy in the company of V. A. Tihonov at a name-day party."
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The letter is dated 1892 (L2), so "last year" moves the party to 1891, the same year as the Europe tour. The original puts it in 1892, which fits the recent Shtcheglov party in the opening paragraph.

8. **CLASS**: claim-drift
   **ORIGINAL**: L18 "I began writing in 1879."
   **REVISION**: L18–19 "I began writing while still at school."
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The year is gone, and the revision adds a claim the original never made: that he started writing before he left school. 1879 is also the year he finished, so the original allows for either.

9. **CLASS**: dropped-fact
   **ORIGINAL**: L18–20 "The published collections of my works are: “Motley Tales,” “In the Twilight,” “Stories,” “Surly People,” and a novel, “The Duel.”"
   **REVISION**: L19–20 "Several collections of my stories have been published, along with a novel."
   **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: All five titles and the count of four collections. The reader can no longer name any of his books, including the novel.

10. **CLASS**: dropped-fact

    **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The specific age, thirteen. It is a word-form number, so the scan cannot extract it.
