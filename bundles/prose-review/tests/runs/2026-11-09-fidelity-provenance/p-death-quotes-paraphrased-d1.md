**Findings**

- **CLASS**: dropped-fact
- **ORIGINAL**: line 31: `Augustus Cæsar died in a compliment: “Livia, conjugii nostri memor, vive et vale.”`
- **REVISION**: lines 30–31: `Augustus Cæsar died in a compliment to his wife;`
- **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: Augustus's actual last words ("Livia, mindful of our marriage, live and farewell") are gone, and so is his wife's name. The reader can no longer see what the compliment was.

- **CLASS**: dropped-fact
- **ORIGINAL**: lines 32–33: `Tiberius in dissimulation, as Tacitus saith of him, “Jam Tiberium vires et corpus, non dissimulatio, deserebant:”`
- **REVISION**: line 31: `Tiberius in dissimulation, as Tacitus saith of him;`
- **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: Tacitus's sentence (his strength and body were failing Tiberius, but his dissimulation was not) is gone. "As Tacitus saith of him" now cites Tacitus without anything Tacitus said, so the reader cannot see the evidence for "dissimulation."

- **CLASS**: dropped-fact

- **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The jest itself ("I think I am becoming a god") is gone. The reader is told there was a joke but cannot know what it was or why it counts as dying "in a jest."

- **CLASS**: dropped-fact

- **REVISION**: line 32: `Galba with a sentence, holding forth his neck;`
- **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: Galba's sentence ("Strike, if it be for the good of the Roman people") is gone. The reader learns only that he said something, not what.

- **CLASS**: dropped-fact
- **ORIGINAL**: line 45: `the sweetest canticle is “Nunc dimittis,”`
- **REVISION**: lines 42–43: `the sweetest canticle is the old song of departure,`
- **WHAT THE READER LOSES**: The original names the canticle as the Nunc dimittis, Simeon's song. "The old song of departure" does not say which canticle is meant, so the reader cannot recover the reference.

**Immaterial losses**

- "Pompa mortis magis terret, quam mors ipsa." The revision translates it accurately at lines 13–14 ("the pomp of death frightens us more than death itself"), and the attribution to the philosopher stays.
- "Cogita quamdiu eadem feceris; mori velle, non tantum fortis, aut mi..." Lines 24–26 paraphrase the main clause (courage, misery, tedium). The dropped "think how long you have done the same things" is still carried by the sentence that follows, at lines 26–28.
- "Adeste, si quid mihi restat agendum," Lines 33–34, "calling for whatever remained to be done", keep both the summons and what it was for.
- "qui finem vitæ extremum inter munera ponit naturæ." Lines 36–37, "the man who counts the end of life among the gifts of nature", are an accurate translation, and "saith he" stays.
- "Extinctus amabitur idem." Line 45, "the man once gone will be loved", keeps the meaning. Right before it, "extinguisheth envy" still supplies the "same man" contrast that *idem* carried.

**Scanner defects**

- The scan does not list "Nunc dimittis" (original line 45). It is a quoted span, and it does not appear in the revision. It may have been missed because it is short.
